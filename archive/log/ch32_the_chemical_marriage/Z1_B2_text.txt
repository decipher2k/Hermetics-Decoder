This chapter reads The Chemical Marriage as a ritual narrative of Rosicrucian initiation. Hall treats the seven days as sequential stations of selection, self-knowledge, testing, death, laboratory transformation, ascent, ensoulment, and final investiture. Christian Rosencreutz is the candidate whose humility permits admission where pride fails. The scales reveal true worth, the beheading of the royal figures marks the necessity of sacrificial dissolution, and the work in the tower leads to renewed bodies animated by descending light. The marriage itself is not social ceremony but the union of regenerated human consciousness with its divine source.
