The third cycle resolves the chapter around one governing proposition: the Tabernacle is a disciplined mirror in which cosmic order, human structure, and spiritual ascent are made mutually legible. Hall's purpose is not antiquarian reconstruction but symbolic recovery. The Mosaic sanctuary matters because it preserves a universal science of approach, showing how divine presence is mediated through measured form, graded access, and interior qualification.

Seen from this perspective, every part of the sanctuary becomes dynamic. The court isolates the holy from dispersion; the altar converts sacrifice into self-offering; the laver makes purification a precondition of service; the holy place stabilizes light, nourishment, and voiced aspiration; the holy of holies contracts multiplicity into a veiled center of law and overshadowed presence. The sanctuary is not looked at properly until it is read as process.

The chapter's Egyptological background supports rather than displaces its spiritual claim. Hall uses the parallel to the Mysteries to show continuity of esoteric method: truths too subtle for direct prose are embedded in architecture, vestment, number, and rite. The Tabernacle is therefore best understood as initiatory pedagogy in transportable form.
