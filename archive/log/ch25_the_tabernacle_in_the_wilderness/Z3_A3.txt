The human correspondence now comes into full focus. Hall repeatedly translates sanctuary structure into anatomy and psychology: the body as outer court, the disciplined interior as holy place, the head or innermost consciousness as holy of holies; the larynx as incense altar; the soul-body as divine dwelling; the faculties as bearers of lights, bread, and law. This is not an optional interpretive flourish. It is the basis for the chapter's claim that the true temple is reproduced in the sanctified person.

The same applies to the vestments. The priest is clothed in an externalized diagram of perfected powers. The jewels are ordered multiplicity, the bells rhythmic warning, the pomegranates vital abundance, the crown rule across worlds, the flower-cup receptivity to descending life. Taken together they state that mediation requires a body and psyche made capable of bearing correspondence without distortion.

Yet the stripping away of these signs before the highest entry ensures that the chapter never mistakes symbol for attainment. Representation serves realization, and must finally yield to it.
