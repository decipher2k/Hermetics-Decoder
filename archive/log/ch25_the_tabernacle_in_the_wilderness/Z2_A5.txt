The inner dramatic tension of the chapter lies between manifestation and concealment. The Tabernacle is lavishly specified, yet its goal is the hidden presence beyond image. Ornaments proliferate, yet the center becomes simpler. Robes accumulate, yet the final approach requires stripping. Ritual movement increases in complexity, yet culmination arrives in silent exposure before the unseen. Hall builds the chapter so that form educates the seeker toward what exceeds form.

This gives special force to the ark. It is both container and throne, law chest and point of descent, memory and presence. The cherubim overshadow not an idol but an interval, a seat of communication. The divine is announced through surrounding form, yet never reduced to that form. This is one of the chapter's most subtle theological claims.

The same structure appears in the doctrine of unwritten keys. The written sanctuary needs oral explanation; visible forms need the Qabbalistic key. Hall is therefore arguing that sacred systems always include an exoteric body and an esoteric principle of interpretation.
