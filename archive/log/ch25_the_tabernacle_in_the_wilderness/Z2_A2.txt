Another important refinement is the role of threshold and veiling. The chapter does not imagine reality as flatly available. Each deeper chamber is protected by distance, partition, and qualification. This is not elitism for its own sake but a structural statement: higher realities are not grasped by the same mode that handles outer functions. Different degrees of truth require different degrees of preparation.

The priesthood embodies this law of graduated access. Ordinary priests serve within limited ranges; the High Priest alone reaches the innermost chamber, and even he may enter only under prescribed conditions. The grades of office therefore reflect grades of interior capability. The sanctuary maps a hierarchy of consciousness just as much as a hierarchy of ritual roles.

The chapter repeatedly links these grades to the body. The larynx is compared to the incense altar; the head becomes a holy of holies; vestments externalize inward states. Hall thereby prevents the symbolism from floating away into abstraction. The body itself is a candidate-space in which the temple's structure can be recognized and eventually realized.
