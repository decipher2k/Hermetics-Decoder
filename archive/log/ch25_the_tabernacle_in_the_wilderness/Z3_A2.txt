What is most decisive here is the law of inwardness through threshold. Veils, pillars, courts, and restrictions do not simply exclude; they differentiate. The chapter assumes that reality is stratified and that man must be proportioned to what he seeks. Hall's interpretation of the priestly grades, the partitioned chambers, and the guarded ark all converge on the same point: higher presence requires deeper purification, greater integration, and stricter truthfulness.

This law of threshold also explains the role of symbol itself. A visible form can safely intimate what cannot yet be directly borne. The sanctuary both reveals and protects. Its richness is pedagogical, but its concealment is merciful. One is taught by passing inward, not by abolishing the stages.

Because of this, the chapter belongs closely to the book's larger claim that esoteric systems are compassionate technologies of transmission. They give enough form for approach while withholding enough to prevent profanation.
