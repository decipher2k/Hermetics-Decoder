The chapter's treatment of materials and numbers provides its ontological precision. Gold signifies spiritualized nature, silver regenerated humanity, brass the mixed constitution of ordinary man. Linen, color, wood, water, and fire all mark distinct states of receptivity and operation. Likewise, numerical forms are not incidental counts but structuring intelligences: three for graded worlds, four for elemental manifestation, seven for complete operative order, twelve for totality articulated in cyclical distribution, seventy-two for expanded cosmic division.

This means the Tabernacle is built from intelligible differences. Hall's symbolism is strongest where he shows that matter, number, function, and ritual action are coordinated expressions of one law. The sanctuary becomes a matrix in which ontology, cosmology, and ethics are visibly cross-referenced.

That integration is why the chapter remains so important to the wider project. It provides a concrete model of how the book wants its symbols read: never as isolated images, always as nodes in a total patterned field.
