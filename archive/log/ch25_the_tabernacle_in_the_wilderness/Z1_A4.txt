What sharpens in this pass is the relation between visible craft and invisible presence. Materials are not neutral. Gold, silver, brass, linen, color, precious stones, wood, and fire each register a distinct level of refinement. Matter becomes meaningful according to what it can bear, reflect, or transmit. The sanctuary is thus an ontology of substances as well as a theology of worship.

The chapter also places unusual weight on mediation through lawful sequence. No one simply appears before the divine center. One must pass by stages, and each stage corrects or disciplines a different layer of being. Hall thereby opposes every fantasy of immediate access detached from purification and order. The approach to the inner chamber is not emotional intensity but graduated consecration.

The priestly apparatus makes this point concrete. The breastplate and shoulder stones place celestial and tribal order upon the body; the bells and pomegranates bind sound to fertility and warning; the miter and floral vessel at the crown signify receptivity to descending life. Office is therefore the embodiment of correspondences. The priest is made into a living version of the sanctuary he serves.
