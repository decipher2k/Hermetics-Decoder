Another refinement is the chapter's account of scripture. Hall stresses the Rosicrucian insistence that the Bible is the greatest of books, yet its true and eternal meaning is rarely grasped by sectarian theologians or scholars blinded by their own opinions. This places Rosicrucianism within a hermeneutics of depth rather than rejection. The brethren are not anti-scriptural; they are anti-superficial.

This is important because it joins the fraternity to the book's wider theory of sacred texts. Scripture, like nature, contains inner layers available only to disciplined interpretation. The Rosicrucian path therefore requires not only mystical aspiration but exegetical purification.

Their sacred language and scriptural method are really two sides of the same principle: truth is written everywhere, but only a transformed reader can read it rightly.
