The three mountains provide the clearest final scheme of ascent. Sophia grounds the seeker in the lawful study of nature. Qabbalah teaches him the celestial speech by which relations among worlds become intelligible. Magia crowns the movement with direct instruction in the true nature of things. Hall includes this triad because it integrates philosophy, symbolism, and revelation into one developmental ladder.

This ladder also disciplines desire. One does not leap to miracle, prophecy, or command. One learns, ascends, purifies, and receives. The Rosicrucian critique of occult greed follows directly from this structure.

As in other major chapters, the path is cumulative: lower stages are not discarded but fulfilled within higher ones.
