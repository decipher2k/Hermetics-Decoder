This chapter shifts from the Rosicrucian problem of origin to the Rosicrucian problem of content. Hall warns that reliable information about the fraternity's actual doctrines remains obscured, yet he argues that its exoteric writings, especially the Confessio Fraternitatis, disclose enough of the outer shadow to infer something of the inner teaching. The chapter is therefore a reconstruction of Rosicrucian aims through their public veils.

One major claim stabilizes immediately: there were likely two Rosicrucian bodies, an inner organization whose true identity remained concealed and an outer organization that circulated texts, rumors, and decoys. The manifestoes thus become strategic clouds. Their purpose is not simply to reveal doctrine, but to conceal the center behind controlled disclosures, misleading appearances, and the confusion created by imitators.

From this basis Hall extracts a coherent program. The Rosicrucians appear as reformers of philosophy, religion, government, science, and medicine; guardians of a sacred language of nature and revelation; advocates of graded initiation; and defenders of the principle that all genuine power must remain subordinate to virtue and divine will.
