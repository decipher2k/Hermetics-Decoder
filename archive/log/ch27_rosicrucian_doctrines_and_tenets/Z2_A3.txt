The inner and outer Rosicrucian bodies now take on fuller meaning. The outer body issues writings, attracts impostors, and permits confusion; the inner body remains unseen and uses the confusion as cover. Hall suggests that concealment operates by managed excess. Too much noise around the order protects the silence at its center.

This is a notable strategy of transmission. Instead of relying only on hiddenness by absence, the fraternity hides by overproduction of ambiguous signs. Manifestoes, rumors, commercial frauds, and interpretive disputes become part of the defensive atmosphere.

The doctrine of clouds is therefore sophisticated. Error and partial truth may be intentionally tolerated around the perimeter so that the core remains inviolable.
