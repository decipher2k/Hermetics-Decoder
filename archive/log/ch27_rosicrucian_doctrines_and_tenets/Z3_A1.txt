The third cycle resolves the chapter around one central principle: Rosicrucian doctrine is a science of total reordering whose outward statements are deliberately incomplete because its real subject is the lawful distribution of transformative knowledge. Hall reads the manifestoes as instruments of selection. They proclaim broadly, but they disclose proportionally.

This explains the chapter's paradoxical tone. The brethren invite all nations, offer their treasure freely, and announce a new era of light; at the same time they insist that the unworthy cannot even perceive them. Universality and restriction are not contradictions here. They are two sides of the same initiatory logic: truth is for all in destination, but only for the prepared in reception.

Rosicrucian doctrine is therefore inseparable from pedagogy. It teaches by measure.
