The inner structure of that pedagogy depends on reading. God writes in stars, in nature, in scripture, and in the constitution of man. The fraternity's sacred language is not merely a code but the recovery of a mode of cognition adequate to this script. Hall thereby places Rosicrucianism within a universal hermeneutic: all worlds are legible, but only if man learns to read before he seeks to command.

This is why pseudo-alchemists and false philosophers are so dangerous in the chapter. They mimic the appearance of hidden knowledge while severing symbol from reality. Their complexity and pride contrast with the Rosicrucian insistence that truth, though veiled, is ultimately simple.

The chapter thus draws a sharp line between generative obscurity and corrupt obscurantism.
