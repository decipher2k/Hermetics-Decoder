The chapter's political material should be read structurally rather than merely polemically. Hall presents the Rosicrucians as opposing despotism and seeking the rule of philosophic elect, even connecting them with major revolutionary transitions. Whatever one makes of the historical claims, the underlying doctrine is clear: social order should be governed by intelligence aligned with natural and divine law rather than by inherited power or corrupt priestcraft.

This political aim mirrors the individual's inner work. Just as reason and spiritual insight must govern the lower elements within man, wise principle must govern collective life. Rosicrucian politics is thus anthropology externalized.

The chapter therefore ties personal regeneration directly to civic reform. Both are matters of rightful hierarchy.
