The chapter's ethical severity becomes clearer here. Hall stresses that even real powers such as transmutation and healing are secondary and potentially corrupting. Wealth, dignity, and miracle-working attract temptation. Without prior wisdom they become instruments of destruction. The fraternity therefore reverses the ordinary order of desire: it refuses to make power the proof of attainment.

This is a major clarification of Rosicrucianism as Hall understands it. The fraternity does not deny the possibility of marvels, but it regards pursuit of marvels for their own sake as a diversion from the true work. Virtue, knowledge, and understanding must come first, after which other capacities may be added without spiritual disaster.

The chapter thereby aligns Rosicrucianism with the broader ethical discipline of the book's esotericism. Hidden science is licit only in purified hands.
