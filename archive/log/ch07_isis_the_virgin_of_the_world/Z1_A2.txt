Extraction

Central statements
- Isis gathers many traditions because she names a principle, not a local figure only.
- The chapter resists literal reduction of myth into history.

Operative qualities
Principial unity, trans-cultural recurrence, symbolic breadth, and anti-literalism.

Reformulation

The chapter insists that Isis should be understood as a principle rather than as a merely local or historical figure. This is why she appears under so many names and among so many peoples. Her breadth of attribution is itself evidence that the ancients were tracking a recurrent structure of reality through varying symbolic languages.

This also explains the chapter's impatience with attempts to reduce her to historical queens, genealogies, or simple lunar mythology. Such reductions miss her real scale. She names a universal field of receptive generativity, hidden wisdom, and transformative nurture.
