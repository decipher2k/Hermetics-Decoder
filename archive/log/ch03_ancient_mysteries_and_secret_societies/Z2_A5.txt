Central statements
- The chapter treats initiatory schools as communities that manage threshold-crossing.
- The repeated drama is movement from unfitness to fitness.
- Institutional remnants preserve threshold-techniques.

Operative qualities
Threshold-crossing, managed passage, fitness, guarded admission, and remnant technique.

Reformulation

The Mystery schools presented in this chapter are communities organized around threshold-crossing. They exist to manage the passage from one mode of being to another: from unfitness to fitness, from surface to depth, from confusion to orientation, from symbolic death to restored life.

Their rites differ in content but agree in threshold logic. Something must be left behind. Something must be endured. Something hidden must be approached under condition. And something new must be conferred only after proof.

The Druidic traditions stage this through long preparation, rank, natural symbolism, and moral scrutiny. The Mithraic through martialized confrontation and graduated ascent. The Gnostic through concealed interpretation and layered reality. The Serapic through descent into hidden chambers and confrontation with concentrated sacred force. The northern through traversing worlds and returning as one changed. The Eleusinian through lower sorrow and higher illumination. The Orphic and Bacchic through loss, scattering, and recollected wholeness.

This threshold structure is the chapter's most durable comparative insight. Even where myths diverge widely, the institutional action remains recognizable.

Corruption then appears as failed threshold management. Either the unfit are admitted too easily, or the rite ceases to awaken what it once tested. The gate remains, but no true crossing occurs.

Even then, techniques survive in remnant form. A later fraternity may still possess threshold devices—degrees, veils, blind passages, solemn vows, symbols of death and recovery—even if it no longer remembers the full metaphysical framework that once justified them.

The chapter thus pushes the reader toward a structural history of initiation. What passes from age to age is often not a whole creed, but a set of threshold-techniques for managing transformation.
