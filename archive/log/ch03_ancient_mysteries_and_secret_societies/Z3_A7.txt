Reformulation

The chapter's final stable meaning is not antiquarian but architectural. It explains how ancient institutions preserved hidden law by dramatizing transformation through graded ritual form.

Each tradition contributes a local variation. Druidic, Mithraic, Gnostic, Serapic, northern, Eleusinian, Orphic, and Bacchic schools differ in symbol and theology. Yet all organize access, prepare candidates, stage ordeal, conceal stronger teaching, and seek some form of restored standing through symbolic death and return.

The chapter's true continuity therefore lies in mechanism. These are parallel systems of guarded transformation.

Their myths function as operative symbols, their ceremonies as developmental sequences, and their institutions as containers for selective transmission.

Their historical danger is equally shared. When inward purpose decays, the shell survives longer than the force. But because force was embedded in shell, remnants can still carry traces forward.

The chapter's lasting claim is that ancient wisdom did not survive only in texts or ideas. It survived in ritual architectures capable of outliving the cultures that first generated them.
