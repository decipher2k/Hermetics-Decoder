Reformulation

The chapter now reads most strongly as an institutional account of how transformative teachings survive. Ancient secret schools preserve wisdom by structuring access, testing readiness, and embedding doctrine in durable symbolic forms.

The opening distinction between stronger and weaker minds establishes the need for such institutions. Public religion gives the many an accessible form. Initiatory orders lead the prepared few deeper.

The traditions surveyed then become variations on one architecture. Druidic, Mithraic, Gnostic, Serapic, northern, Eleusinian, Orphic, and Bacchic schools all combine selective admission, symbolic passage, moral proving, hidden instruction, and some form of death-and-return.

Their myths differ, but their work is similar: they stage the soul's captivity, struggle, purification, recollection, and possible restoration.

Their corruption also follows a common law: once inward seriousness declines, outer form remains but inner force thins.

Yet because the force was embedded in design, remnants endure. Later ritual bodies may preserve the architecture of transformation even after losing much of the original doctrinal fullness.

The chapter thus gives the reader a stable interpretive key. Read these schools as structurally related experiments in preserving hidden law through ritual form, and their diversity becomes ordered rather than chaotic.
