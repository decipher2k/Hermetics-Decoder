Reformulation

The chapter can also be read as a comparative science of concealment and revelation. Concealment is not treated as arbitrary exclusion. It is presented as a necessary architecture for developmental truth.

A teaching too strong for the unprepared can be misunderstood, degraded, or weaponized. Therefore the Mysteries hide not from hatred of the many, but from regard for the conditions under which transformation is possible.

This protective concealment becomes architectural. It is built into degrees, passwords, vows, chambers, symbolic tests, ritual objects, and staged disclosures. The candidate does not stand outside the teaching and inspect it all at once. He advances into it by becoming capable of more of it.

The chapter's traditions all express this architecture. Druidic modes preserve oral depth under severe discipline. Mithraic modes reveal through rank and ordeal. Gnostic modes reveal through hidden interpretation. Serapic modes reveal through space, drama, and controlled awe. Northern modes reveal through dangerous passage. Eleusinian modes reveal through progressive disclosure culminating in vision. Orphic and Bacchic modes reveal through symbols of brokenness and restored inner life.

Read this way, the chapter clarifies the relation between secrecy and wisdom. Secrecy is not the point; ordered revelation is the point.

Corruption occurs when concealment remains after its developmental purpose has been forgotten. Then secrecy becomes mere exclusiveness, and revelation becomes empty spectacle.

But because the architecture of revelation can survive longer than its explanation, later institutions may still transmit partial depth. The chapter therefore traces not just ancient secrecy, but the long historical afterlife of developmental form.
