Reformulation

The chapter reaches its highest comparative clarity when it is read for repeated sequence rather than shared dogma. The ancient schools do not all teach the same verbal theology, but they repeatedly enact the same formal movement: separation, ordeal, revelation, and reintegration.

The candidate is set apart from ordinary standing. He passes through controlled danger, disorientation, or symbolic death. Hidden meaning is disclosed under condition. He is then restored under a new relation to law, community, and self.

This sequence is visible across the chapter's traditions. Druidic preparation and rank, Mithraic grades and combats, Gnostic veils and recoveries, Serapic descents and central revelations, northern world-passages, Eleusinian sorrow and illumination, Orphic fragmentation and recollection, Bacchic rupture and ecstatic reintegration all belong to one broad ritual grammar.

The chapter's historical subtlety lies in preserving difference while tracking this grammar. No tradition is erased; each becomes more legible through the function it serves in the shared pattern.

That shared pattern also explains survival. Formal sequences are easier to transmit across centuries than complete doctrinal systems. A later order may reproduce the movement of separation, ordeal, disclosure, and reintegration even after the original metaphysics has faded.

The chapter therefore shows how ritual patterns become carriers of hidden continuity. The old wisdom survives not only in propositions, but in enacted sequences that continue to shape human transformation.
