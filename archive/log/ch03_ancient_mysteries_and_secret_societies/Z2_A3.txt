Reformulation

The chapter treats myth as operational symbolism. A story of descent, betrayal, capture, wandering, dismemberment, or return is not only something to be believed or admired. It is a formal device by which a school encodes law and stages the candidate's passage through its implications.

This is why initiation is sequential. One does not receive the final teaching first. The candidate must move through ordered phases that correspond to conditions of the soul: ignorance, confusion, testing, fidelity, terror, partial insight, and renewed standing.

The Druidic path encodes this through grades, colors, sacred timing, and increasingly interior instruction. The Mithraic path encodes it through conflict, mediation, armor, descent, and exalted return. The Gnostic path encodes it through layers of emanation, hidden names, and the recovery of relation to higher power. The Serapic path encodes it through labyrinthine descent, engineered trial, and confrontation with centralized sacred presence. The northern path encodes it through world-crossing and the winning of wisdom through symbolic death. The Eleusinian path encodes it through separation, search, lower captivity, and direct sight. The Orphic and Bacchic paths encode it through fragmentation, hidden center, sacrifice, and recollection.

In all of these forms, law is not merely spoken. It is enacted. The initiate learns by entering a model of reality strong enough to dislocate old perception.

The chapter's warning about corruption now appears as a warning about form emptied of function. When the rite remains but no longer transforms, when secrecy remains but no longer protects wisdom, when symbols remain but no longer disclose law, institutional decay has already occurred.

Yet the same formal logic that makes the Mysteries fragile also makes them durable. Even when doctrine thins, the architecture of transmission may survive. Graded access, solemn vows, symbolic death, restored light, hidden teaching, sacred implements, moral screening, and rebirth language can all carry remnants of the older pattern into later ages.

The surviving significance of the Mysteries lies partly here. They may not always persist as complete doctrinal systems, but their formal logic of transformation can continue through later brotherhoods and ceremonial traditions.

The chapter therefore trains the reader to recognize durable sequence beneath changing imagery. Different myths, one recurrent initiation-pattern.
