This chapter presents Freemasonry as a symbolic heir to ancient builder Mysteries. Hall traces Masonic emblems through primeval pillars, Enochian vaults, Dionysiac architects, temple traditions, and the cosmic meaning of proportion. Architecture becomes the governing analogy: as the universe is built, as the body is ordered, and as the soul is fashioned, so the initiate must labor upon the rough ashlar of himself and of society. Solomon's Temple is treated not merely as a building in history but as the house of universal light, the human body, and the invisible soul-temple. The chapter's deeper claim is that Masonic symbolism survives because it still bears the fragments of an initiatory science of sacred construction.
