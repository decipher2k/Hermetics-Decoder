This chapter interprets Freemasonry as the surviving university of a far older sacred architectonics. Hall links its symbols to initiatory builder traditions that saw proportion, geometry, music, astronomy, and temple construction as one science of forming both structures and souls. The visible temple, the human body, and the invisible soul-house are treated as parallel versions of the same design. Freemasonry matters because its stories and emblems still preserve a grammar of transformation, even where the literal institution has forgotten the full content of what it carries.
