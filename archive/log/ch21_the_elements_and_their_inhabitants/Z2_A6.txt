Hall uses the doctrine of elementals to articulate a layered and differentiated invisible ecology. The four elements each have subtle interiors, and within those interiors dwell specialized beings whose functions correspond to mineral, fluid, fiery, and aerial processes. These beings are neither immortal spirits nor evil demons by default, but intermediate forms of life with their own limits, rulers, habitats, and modes of intelligence.

The chapter's deeper force lies in its insistence that subtle worlds must be discriminated carefully. Invisible life is not one thing, and only a composite being like man can stand at the crossing of multiple elemental domains.
