This chapter develops a subtle ecology of the cosmos by distinguishing the elemental kingdoms and assigning to each a proper field of action. Earth, water, fire, and air are understood as possessing invisible ethers as well as visible bodies, and within those ethers dwell beings whose one-sided but highly specialized constitutions make them experts in a single domain of Nature.

Gnomes govern hidden solidity and growth from below; undines the living fluidity and beautifying powers of moisture; salamanders the fierce and transformative agencies of heat; sylphs the volatile and inspiriting movements of air and ether. Because man contains relations to all four, he can serve as a crossing point of these worlds, though seldom without confusion.

The chapter insists that invisible beings must be classified rather than indiscriminately feared. Elementals are neither angels nor demons simply by being unseen. They are lawful but limited forms of life whose existence makes Nature appear organized by subtler intelligences.
