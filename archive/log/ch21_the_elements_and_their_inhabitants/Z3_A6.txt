Final recomposition draft:

The chapter reads the elements as having both visible substance and invisible essence, and it places within those subtler elemental interiors the beings long known as Nature spirits. Gnomes, undines, salamanders, and sylphs are not presented as mere poetic ornaments, but as intermediate forms of life specialized to one domain of the cosmos and one style of process. Their one-sided composition makes them limited, but also precise in function.

Through them Hall reinterprets mineral formation, plant vitality, water movement, atmospheric change, heat, inspiration, and many other natural processes as expressions of localized intelligences working in lawful relation to the four elements. Because man partakes of all four elemental fields, he alone among embodied beings can stand at their crossing and imperfectly commune with them.

The chapter's deeper teaching is ontological and methodological alike: reality is layered, the invisible world is differentiated, and symbolic personification can preserve truths about process that dead mechanism tends to obscure.
