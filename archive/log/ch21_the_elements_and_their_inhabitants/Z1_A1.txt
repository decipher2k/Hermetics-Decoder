Chapter 21 presents the elemental world as a missing middle realm between gross matter and higher spiritual hierarchies. Its main task is to make Nature porous again: what seems materially inert or merely physical is re-described as inhabited, coordinated, and animated by specialized intelligences operating through subtler grades of the same elements.

The chapter begins by splitting each element into gross and subtle phases. Earth, water, air, and fire are not simple substances but outward bodies of inward essences. From that premise Hall, following Paracelsus, introduces the Nature spirits as beings constituted of one elemental ether and functioning wholly within it. These beings are not immortal spirits, yet neither are they merely material creatures. They are intermediate, specialized, and locally intelligent.

The chapter is not only classificatory. It teaches a model of distributed intelligence in Nature. Gnomes, undines, salamanders, and sylphs become the hidden laborers through whom mineral growth, vegetation, fluid movement, atmospheric change, warmth, inspiration, and countless natural operations are rendered symbolically legible.
