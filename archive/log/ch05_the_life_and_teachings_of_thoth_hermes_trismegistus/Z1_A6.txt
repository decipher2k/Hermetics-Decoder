Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter binds contemplation to mission.
- Revelation culminates in service, not private illumination alone.
- True sanctification includes enlightening others.

Operative qualities
Consecration, mission, service, sanctifying speech, and transmissive responsibility.

Reformulation

The chapter does not end in private ecstasy. Hermes consecrates his life to the service of the great light, preaches to humanity, gathers disciples, writes the vision, and commands that the doctrines be preserved inviolate. The revealed one becomes the responsible transmitter.

This gives the chapter ethical force. Illumination that does not become service is incomplete. To know the light truly is to assist others in rising from ignorance, provided they are capable of hearing.

The closing hymn reinforces this. Praise, silence, purity, knowledge, and the sanctification of others all belong together. Wisdom is not possession; it is participation in a living current that seeks continuation.
