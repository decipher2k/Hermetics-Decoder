Z2_A4

Extraction

Central statements
- The human problem is reflective entrapment.
- Bondage arises through fascination with likeness rather than direct reality.

Operative qualities
Reflective entrapment, likeness, fascination, bondage, and recovery of orientation.

Reformulation

The chapter's anthropology becomes sharper when stated this way: man is bound because he mistakes likeness for reality. The luminous human principle descends by loving its own shadow. Reflection becomes captivity.

This is a subtle and central idea. Evil is not first described as external invasion but as inward confusion. Salvation therefore begins as recovery of orientation: learning again what in oneself is light and what is borrowed obscurity.
