Extraction

Central statements
- Poimandres reveals a cosmology of light, mind, word, and descending mixture.
- Creation is lawful emanation, not arbitrary manufacture.
- Man's fall is attraction to reflected image.

Operative qualities
Emanation, archetypal order, reflective entanglement, lawful creation, and divided being.

Reformulation

The chapter's metaphysical core lies in Poimandres. The universe begins not with inert matter but with light and mind. Darkness and substance appear as derivative conditions. Reason, the word born of mind, divides and orders, establishes truth amidst turbulence, and makes visible what would otherwise remain hidden.

Creation is therefore not random production. It is emanative and archetypal. The pattern exists before the visible world; the workman shapes becoming according to forms held in higher intelligence.

The human fall occurs when the luminous man beholds his own image reflected below and desires it. This is one of the chapter's most important formulations. Error is not sheer rebellion but fascinated misidentification. The immortal enters the lower image, becomes composite, and suffers under fate because it mistakes reflection for reality.

The chapter's anthropology follows directly from this. The human being is mixed: inwardly of light and life, outwardly entangled with elemental and planetary conditions. The spiritual task is therefore discriminative. One must separate self from not-self and recover relation to origin.
