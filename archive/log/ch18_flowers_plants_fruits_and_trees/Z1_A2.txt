First reduction shows six major symbolic operations.

1. Vegetal generation makes divine creativity discussable without vulgarity.
2. Flowering represents purity and unfoldment rather than mere reproduction.
3. The lotus and rose link body, psyche, and spiritual awakening through petaled centers.
4. The tree serves as both macrocosm and microcosm.
5. Fruits and herbs condense moral, magical, medicinal, or initiatory properties.
6. Plant symbols repeatedly join usefulness and metaphysical meaning.

The chapter therefore develops a symbolic botany in which growth itself becomes theology.
