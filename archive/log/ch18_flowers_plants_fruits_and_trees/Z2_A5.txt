The chapter also strengthens a methodological principle running across the book: usefulness and symbolism are not opposed. Trees are sacred partly because they shelter, heal, nourish, and build. Herbs are symbolically charged partly because they alter consciousness or cure disease. Plant symbolism often begins in close observation of effect and then widens into metaphysical interpretation.

This matters because Hall is not constructing arbitrary correspondences. He repeatedly roots symbolic value in function, process, and sensible relation.
