Chapter 18 initially appears to move from sexual symbolism to floral, arboreal, and vegetal symbolism, but its deeper unity is clear: plant life is treated as the most explicit natural revelation of generation, unfoldment, nourishment, polarity, and immortality. Flowers, fruits, seeds, and trees are the visible liturgy of the creative mystery.

The opening defense of phallic and yonic symbolism sets the governing tone. Hall insists that generative symbols become obscene only to consciousness that has lost symbolic understanding. In the Mysteries, generation is sacred because it mirrors the divine process by which spirit enters matter and life comes forth. Plant symbolism therefore becomes a purified language for discussing creation, reproduction, chastity, unfoldment, and resurrection.

The lotus and the rose emerge as central forms, uniting maternal mystery, spiritual centers, and the soul's ascent from mud through water into light. The tree then widens the scale from body and blossom to cosmos and genealogy, becoming the diagram of emanation, lineage, support, equilibrium, and the axis between worlds.
