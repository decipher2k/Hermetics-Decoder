Second pass clarifies that the chapter hinges on the rehabilitation of generative symbolism. Hall argues that sacred botany is impossible to understand unless one first restores reproductive imagery to dignity. Plant symbolism works precisely because it reveals sexuality, fertility, and birth under the aspect of law, beauty, and continuity rather than appetite alone.

This places the chapter in continuity with earlier material on body, harmony, and sacred craft. Generation is not an embarrassing biological fact but a universal mode of manifestation. The vegetal world becomes its most pedagogically gentle expression.
