Final transmutation:

Hall's botany is a theology of becoming. Plants teach that life rises lawfully, differentiates without losing origin, and carries generative fire into forms of nourishment, beauty, medicine, and seed. What the flower displays in brief, the tree sustains in full: a hidden root feeding a visible order that expands, shelters, and bears fruit. Because plant life joins fecundity and innocence more visibly than animal life, it becomes the privileged symbolic medium for mysteries of purity, creation, and rebirth.

The chapter therefore turns the garden, grove, blossom, fruit, and branch into a complete initiatory grammar.
