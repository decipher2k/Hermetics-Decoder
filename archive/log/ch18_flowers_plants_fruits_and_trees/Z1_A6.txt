Recomposition sketch:

Hall presents flowers, fruits, and trees as one of the most refined symbolic languages of the ancient world. The generative mystery, often misunderstood by modern moralism, is here elevated through plant imagery into a doctrine of sacred creativity. The flower signifies reproductive purity and spiritual unfoldment; the lotus and rose especially image the awakening of hidden centers and the soul's ascent from darkness into illumination. The tree expands this symbolism into cosmology and anthropology, serving as a figure of emanation, rooted nourishment, branching diversity, family descent, resurrection, and the vertical relation between worlds. Sacred fruits and herbs further condense mysteries of fecundity, temptation, ecstasy, healing, immortality, and magical agency.

The chapter's inner law is that growth itself is a revelation of the divine order.
