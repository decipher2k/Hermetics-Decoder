Final recomposition draft:

The chapter reads flowers, plants, fruits, and trees as the most serene natural exposition of divine creativity. Ancient symbolism turns to vegetal life because it reveals, with unusual clarity, how hidden potency enters matter, unfolds by degrees, flowers into beauty, bears fruit, and renews itself through seed and season. The generative mystery is therefore not degraded but sanctified.

Within this field, the lotus and rose symbolize the opening of latent spiritual centers and the ascent of consciousness from obscurity into light. The tree becomes the great diagram of unity-in-multiplicity: rooted in invisible nourishment, rising along a central axis, and branching into countless forms while remaining one life. Sacred species, fruits, and magical herbs further articulate immortality, equilibrium, healing, ecstasy, temptation, fecundity, and initiatory continuity.

Plant symbolism here is not ornamental. It is a science of growth through which Nature displays the laws of manifestation and return.
