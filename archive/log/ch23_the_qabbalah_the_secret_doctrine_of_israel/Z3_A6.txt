Final recomposition draft:

The chapter reads the Qabbalah as the hidden doctrine through which Israel's sacred tradition, the structure of creation, and the constitution of man become mutually intelligible. Law has an outer form, an inner soul, and a still deeper interior; scripture is therefore a veil over metaphysical architecture. The Sepher Yetzirah, the doctrine of the unconditioned source, the four worlds, the forty spheres, and the Sephirothic Tree all describe one ordered process by which the undefined gives rise to defined existence through graded emanation, reflected power, and progressive limitation.

Within this process, letters, numbers, and names are not arbitrary human devices but operative principles of formation. The Tree of Sephiroth condenses the whole order into ten powers and twenty-two paths arranged in balanced columns, revealing how polarity, mediation, and hierarchy sustain both cosmos and consciousness. Adam Kadmon and the microcosmic human being then show that man carries the same structure within himself, though in contracted and divided form. The fall signifies descent into denser embodiment; the hidden paths remain because the higher order still interpenetrates the lower.

The chapter's deepest teaching is that decipherment is ascent. Gematria, Notarikon, and Temurah exemplify the larger principle that truth is concealed within its own garments and must be restored by disciplined symbolic reading. Qabbalah is thus both a map of the world and a method for returning through its veils toward the living source.
