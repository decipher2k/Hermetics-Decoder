This chapter presents the Qabbalah as the hidden doctrine within Israel's sacred tradition and as a symbolic system capable of explaining both creation and revelation. Hall describes it as the soul of the soul of the law: the interior teaching concealed within scripture, ritual, and inherited doctrine. The Sepher Yetzirah and the later Sephirothic teachings show how the world is formed through the graded activity of numbers, letters, sounds, and divine names.

At the center of the chapter is a doctrine of emanation. From the unconditioned and indefinable source, increasingly limited worlds, powers, hierarchies, and substances emerge by reflection and differentiation. The four worlds, the forty spheres, the ten Sephiroth, and the twenty-two paths together form a structured map of descent from unity into multiplicity. The same pattern is mirrored in man, who as microcosm contains the entire order in reduced form.

The chapter also insists that sacred texts must be read as layered constructions. Gematria, Notarikon, and Temurah are not games but methods for penetrating beyond literal surface. Qabbalah becomes the art of recovering hidden order wherever it has been veiled--in scripture, in cosmos, and in man himself.
