Extraction

Central statements
- Hearing and sight are trained here as symbolic faculties.
- Sensation becomes a doorway to proportion when rightly educated.
- Perception is reformable.

Operative qualities
Educated perception, symbolic sensing, refined receptivity, and sensory initiation.

Reformulation

Another mature line in the chapter is its pedagogy of perception. Hearing and sight are not treated as neutral receivers. They must be educated to recognize proportion, dignity, and effect. Only then do sound and color become true teachers.

This means the chapter is also about the reform of sensation. The senses may be captive to coarseness, or they may become initiatory doors through which hidden order is consciously received.

Such education explains the concern with noble music, sacred songs, planetary vowels, and disciplined color-systems.

The chapter therefore presents perception itself as a field of initiation.
