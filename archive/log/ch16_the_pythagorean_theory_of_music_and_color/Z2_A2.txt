Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter binds sense and reason rather than surrendering one to the other.
- Harmony must be mathematically known and experientially felt.
- Formal law and lived effect cooperate.

Operative qualities
Rational sensing, measured experience, dual validation, and integrated knowing.

Reformulation

The Canonics-Harmonics distinction becomes clearer here. The chapter sides with reason and mathematics against mere taste, but it does not eliminate experience. Rather, it insists that true harmony is both formally lawful and experientially potent.

This integration is one of the chapter's strongest methodological points. Sound and color must be measured, yet their value is verified in their effects upon life.

Thus the Pythagorean science avoids two errors: sensuous arbitrariness and lifeless abstraction.

The chapter teaches that authentic order is simultaneously exact and lived.
