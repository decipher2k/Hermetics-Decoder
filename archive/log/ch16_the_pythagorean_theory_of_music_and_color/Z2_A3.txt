Extraction

Central statements
- Music, architecture, and vibration are treated as formally analogous.
- Built form is a kind of frozen chord.
- Sound and shape belong to one harmonic field.

Operative qualities
Cross-modal analogy, crystallized harmony, vibratory architecture, and formal transposition.

Reformulation

The chapter repeatedly extends harmony across media. Architecture is compared to music, sound chambers to vibrational instruments, and built proportions to chords. Form and tone are therefore not separate arts but transpositions of one law.

This is why temples, ritual utterances, and structural resonance matter so much. A building can carry harmony, store it, and even release it under proper conditions.

The chapter thereby enriches the earlier temple symbolism by adding vibratory depth. Form is sound rendered spatially; sound is structure rendered temporally.

Harmony becomes a cross-modal science of creation.
