Central statements
- The chapter presents harmony as the immediate prerequisite of beauty and the manifest expression of the Good.
- Music and color are treated as lawful expressions of proportion rather than subjective ornament.
- Pythagorean theory links aesthetics to cosmology, ethics, and healing.

Operative qualities
Harmony, proportional beauty, lawful manifestation, and aesthetic intelligibility.

Reformulation

This chapter presents harmony as one of the book's most pervasive governing laws. Beauty is not arbitrary attractiveness or mere sensory pleasure. It is the visible or audible expression of proportionate relation, and therefore the manifest action of the Good in the world of form.

From that premise the chapter joins music and color into a single field of lawful correspondence. Both are treated as modes by which hidden order becomes perceptible. Tone and hue do not decorate reality from the outside; they reveal how reality is internally arranged.

The Pythagorean contribution is decisive because it gives this field mathematical dignity. Music and color become exact sciences of ratio, gradation, and cosmic relation.

The chapter should therefore be read as an expansion of number into audible and visible harmony.
