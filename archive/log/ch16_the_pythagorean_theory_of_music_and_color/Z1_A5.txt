Extraction

Central statements
- Music of the spheres universalizes harmony beyond human art.
- Celestial motion, planetary order, and sacred vowels are read sonically.
- Cosmos itself is portrayed as ordered chant.

Operative qualities
Cosmic chant, celestial proportion, universal resonance, and sonic cosmology.

Reformulation

The section on the music of the spheres broadens the chapter decisively. Harmony no longer belongs only to instruments or human composition. The cosmos itself is imagined as a monochord, a vast ordered sounding in which planets, heavens, and sacred vowels participate.

This does not reduce astronomy to fantasy. It expresses the conviction that motion, ratio, and order are intrinsically musical. The universe sings because it is proportioned.

The chapter thereby makes sound one of the primary metaphors and realities of cosmic intelligibility.

Celestial order becomes audible, at least to the soul restored to proper correspondence.
