Central statements
- The chapter increasingly reads as a science of attunement.
- Harmony is not static symmetry but active right relation.
- Sound and color tune soul, body, and perception toward order.

Operative qualities
Attunement, active proportion, perceptual tuning, and restorative relation.

Reformulation

On second pass, the chapter's center shifts toward attunement. Harmony is not simply the abstract presence of correct ratios. It is the active bringing-into-right-relation of parts that might otherwise remain inert, fragmented, or disordered.

This is why music and color matter so deeply. They are not merely examples of harmony; they are means by which the soul and body may be tuned toward it.

The chapter therefore becomes a manual of perceptual and affective alignment. To hear rightly and see rightly is already to participate more deeply in order.

Harmony becomes formative rather than merely descriptive.
