Extraction

Central statements
- Pythagoras raises music to exact science through ratio and interval.
- Tetractys, monochord, and diatonic relations provide formal basis.
- Harmony is mathematically grounded and therefore cosmically transferable.

Operative qualities
Ratio, formal exactness, transferable law, and measured consonance.

Reformulation

The chapter makes Pythagoras central by showing how he translated musical experience into formal law. Harmonic intervals are not left to taste. They are discovered as ratios, structured by exact numerical relations and embodied in the tetractys and monochord.

This mathematical grounding matters because it allows harmony to travel beyond music. Once interval is known as law, it can be recognized in the planets, the elements, the body, and the arrangement of the sciences.

The diatonic scale becomes a revelation of deeper proportion, not simply a useful pattern of notes.

The chapter therefore extends Pythagorean mathematics into the realm of audible order.
