Extraction

Central statements
- Music is treated as a structural analogue of the human constitution.
- Instrument, string, and musician correspond to body, nerve, and spirit.
- Sound becomes a model of normal and disordered functioning.

Operative qualities
Embodied resonance, analogical instrumentation, living vibration, and functional consonance.

Reformulation

The chapter's early teaching on the lyre reveals that music is not only external sound. The instrument itself becomes a model of man: body as frame, nerves as strings, spirit as musician. Harmony is the sign of rightly ordered functioning; discord marks defilement or imbalance.

This analogy is crucial because it makes music immediately anthropological. The science of tone is a science of the human being in motion and relation.

The chapter thereby continues the book's larger use of embodiment while shifting from symbolic anatomy to symbolic vibration.

Music becomes the audible image of inward order.
