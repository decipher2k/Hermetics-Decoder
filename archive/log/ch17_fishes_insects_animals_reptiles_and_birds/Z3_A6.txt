Final recomposition draft:

The chapter reads the kingdoms of life as a graduated symbolic language. Ancient peoples revered creatures because they believed invisible powers disclosed themselves in visible habits more simply than in human life. Fish, insects, reptiles, birds, and animals therefore became emblems not by whim but by observed analogy. Each class of life manifests a dominant mode of force: generation, diffusion, coiling potency, aspiration, sovereignty, sacrifice, burden, fidelity, or transformation.

The chapter's deeper force lies in its refusal of simplification. Creature-symbols remain mobile across cosmological, ritual, bodily, and psychological levels. The serpent can be tempter, healer, eternal energy, and savior; the scorpion can betray or guard wisdom; the phoenix can be bird, sun, initiate, alchemical triumph, or political emblem. Composite creatures intensify this method by making mixed consciousness visible at once.

Nature becomes a field of living hieroglyphs, and symbolic knowledge becomes the art of reading power through form.
