The chapter also sharpens a recurring polarity already present elsewhere in the book: the same force may descend as appetite or ascend as illumination.

The serpent is the clearest instance. Christian polemic makes it evil; the esoteric reading restores it as wisdom, spinal fire, healing, immortality, and universal energy. The scorpion may betray or illuminate. Birds of prey may seem destructive yet purify by disintegration. The bull may be brute fertility or the vehicle of divine presence. Even the fly, symbol of annoyance, becomes a purifier through removal of decay.

This polarity matters because the chapter resists moral simplification. It insists on governance, degree, and use. Symbolic creatures do not divide neatly into holy and profane. They stage the question of what becomes of power under different orders of consciousness.
