Hall uses the animal world to show that ancient symbolism is fundamentally analogical. Creatures are venerated not for themselves alone but because they preserve readable patterns of force. Water-life, swarming life, coiling life, winged life, and burden-bearing life each disclose a distinct mode of relation between visible form and invisible power.

The chapter also reveals that symbols are not fixed moral badges. The same creature may signify danger, wisdom, healing, or rebirth depending on the degree at which its governing force is read. Hybrid beasts intensify this method by deliberately combining functions into single teaching-images. What looks like fantasy is actually structural pedagogy.

The result is a symbolic natural history in which the world of creatures becomes a mirror of the soul's condition and a map of the energies through which it may fall, struggle, or be renewed.
