First transmutation:

The chapter is not about animals. It is about the readability of force. Simpler organisms are treated as purer scripts because their behavior has not yet become inwardly divided the way human life has. Ancient priesthoods therefore read creaturely form as a series of short, vivid equations: water-bearing life, winged ascent, coiled power, swarming corruption, sacrificial nourishment, vigilant sovereignty, cyclic rebirth.

Seen this way, the chapter also explains why myth tolerates hybrid beings. A composite creature is a deliberate overprint of powers. It shows that consciousness is stratified and that man himself is a difficult conjunction of beast, intelligence, aspiration, appetite, and transcendence. The symbolic menagerie is therefore a dispersed anthropology.

Its central thesis is that salvation and danger are carried by the same energies under different governance. The serpent, scorpion, eagle, phoenix, lamb, bull, and dog all show that power is not morally simple; it becomes destructive or redemptive according to ordering.
