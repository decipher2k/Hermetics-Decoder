Second pass confirms that the chapter is organized less by taxonomy than by progressive revelation of mediation. Each kingdom of life offers a different way that force enters form.

Fish reveal emergence from undifferentiated life and the mystery of salvation concealed in immersion. Insects reveal distributed and often invisible agencies: swarming contagion, rhythmic labor, metamorphic unfolding, and minute but immense power. Reptiles reveal the continuity of force through coiling, shedding, and electric movement. Birds reveal elevation and transmission between worlds. Mammals and hybrid beasts reveal the structure of the human condition, in which instinct, nobility, burden, memory, violence, devotion, and spiritual possibility are entangled.

The chapter therefore moves from elemental life toward reflective life. It begins near the generative waters and ends in beings that closely shadow man's own composite state.
