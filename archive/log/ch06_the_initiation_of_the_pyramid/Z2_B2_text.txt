The chapter can now be read as an account of preserved initiation through architecture. The Great Pyramid matters because sacred function was built into durable form strongly enough to survive even after the rites themselves fell silent.

Its geometry is therefore revelatory. Orientation, proportion, number, chamber-relation, and passage sequence all embody intelligible order in inhabitable form. The initiate enters a building that is also a cosmology and an anthropology.

This explains the chapter's stress on hidden entrances, sealed routes, and lost ceremonial ways. The Pyramid was a discipline of thresholds. Access to its highest use was guarded because transformation was staged and sequential.

Its symbolism culminates in two joined images. First, the capstone: the human being inwardly finished into the focal form that completes the temple. Second, the King's Chamber: the site of second death, where metaphysical teaching becomes direct certainty through ordeal.

The chapter's final point is not merely that the Pyramid once housed mysteries, but that it still witnesses to them. Its shell remains meaningful because the rites were compressed into design.
