Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter treats sacred geography as integral to wonder.
- Place is not neutral; selected sites amplify or anchor hidden forces.
- World-order is distributed through nodal locations.

Operative qualities
Sacred placement, nodal geography, localization of force, and spatial correspondence.

Reformulation

Place now emerges as a decisive factor. Delphi, Dodona, Lebadia, Rhodes, Ephesus, Olympia, Alexandria, Babylon, Halicarnassus, and the Great Pyramid are not interchangeable settings. They are charged locations where force is anchored, distributed, or made consultable.

This means that wonder belongs to geography as much as to artifact. Sacred science chooses where to build, where to descend, where to listen, where to enthrone, and where to orient collective memory. Place itself becomes symbolic medium.

The chapter therefore imagines the ancient world as a patterned landscape rather than a random spread of marvels. Certain sites become nodal because they host intensified relations between the human and the cosmic.

This spatial logic prepares the reader to see civilization itself as an arrangement of sacred centers.
