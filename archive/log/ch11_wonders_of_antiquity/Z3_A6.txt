Central statements
- The chapter ultimately opposes disenchantment by recovering operative meaning in remains.
- Skeptical explanation alone cannot account for the symbolic persistence of these forms.
- True interpretation restores relation rather than abolishing marvel.

Operative qualities
Re-enchantment through understanding, restored relation, principial reading, and surviving sacred pressure.

Reformulation

At its strongest, the chapter pushes back against disenchantment. Hall does not deny that mechanisms, fuels, fumes, or construction techniques matter. He denies that such explanations are sufficient once detached from the symbolic and initiatory horizon that gave them purpose.

To understand a perpetual lamp only as chemistry, or Delphi only as gas-induced trance, or a wonder only as engineering, is to recover means while losing end. The sacred dimension lies in the ordered relation between means and transcendent aim.

Interpretation therefore should not abolish marvel. It should refine it. One moves from vague astonishment to lucid recognition that these forms were built to preserve relation with realities later cultures no longer know how to house.

This makes the chapter a defense of meaningful wonder: mystery disciplined by structure rather than destroyed by analysis.
