Extraction

Central statements
- Light, voice, and structure are the chapter's three dominant media.
- Each medium preserves a relation to the invisible by different means.
- The chapter repeatedly shows immaterial meanings carried by material supports.

Operative qualities
Illumination, articulation, embodiment, resonance, and multi-medium transmission.

Reformulation

The chapter can be organized around three media through which antiquity preserved relation to hidden reality: light, voice, and structure. The perpetual lamp preserves light. The oracles preserve inspired voice. The wonders preserve structure on a monumental scale. Together they form a grammar of transmission.

Light is the most immediate sign. It signifies presence, vigilance, continuity, and victory over burial. Voice enters next, but never disembodied. It requires cavern, tree, priestess, brass vessel, or trance-body through which utterance can sound. Structure is the slowest and most durable medium: temple, statue, pyramid, garden, or lighthouse storing order across centuries.

These media reveal the chapter's deeper conviction that invisible realities need not remain abstract. They can inhabit crafted supports without becoming reducible to them. Matter does not cancel mystery; it shelters and conducts it.

This gives the whole chapter unusual coherence. However varied its anecdotes may seem, each one shows a different way ancient cultures built a meeting-place between what passes away and what endures.
