Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter increasingly reads as a study in controlled mediation.
- Ancient marvels depend on interfaces between worlds rather than on brute impossibility.
- The decisive issue is how forces are staged, concentrated, and released.

Operative qualities
Interface, concentration, release, ceremonial engineering, and controlled access.

Reformulation

On second pass, the chapter's center moves from preservation in general to controlled mediation in particular. The wonders matter because they are interfaces. They allow hidden force to persist, speak, or radiate through carefully managed conditions.

The perpetual lamp is a controlled interface between flame and fuel, enclosure and exposure, death-chamber and enduring presence. Its secrecy is practical and symbolic at once. Sudden air extinguishes the light because the threshold conditions that sustained it have been broken.

The oracles intensify this principle. Everything surrounding them is threshold work: purification, drugged or inspired states, selected bodies, resonant containers, sacred trees, subterranean fissures, prescribed offerings, and priestly interpretation. Hidden voice does not simply appear. It is staged through ceremonial engineering.

The Seven Wonders apply similar logic at civilizational scale. Through proportion, placement, and symbolic dedication they concentrate powers the ordinary eye reads only as beauty or enormity. Their real magnitude lies in their success as interfaces between visible order and invisible law.
