Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter moves from isolated marvel to general law of preservation.
- Wonders are intelligible when read as containers of operative relation between spirit, craft, and form.
- Ancient sacred technique appears as exact preparation rather than vague superstition.

Operative qualities
Preparation, containment, threshold-making, exactness, and transmission by construction.

Reformulation

The chapter grows clearer when its episodes are read as variations on preserved operativity. The ever-burning lamp is not merely a strange object; it is a controlled enclosure in which fire, fuel, wick, vessel, and seal cooperate to maintain continuity. The marvel lies in prepared relation.

The oracles reveal the same principle in human form. Cavern, vapor, tripod, purification, scribes, sacred tree, sounding brass, memory-fountain, and priestly interpretation all serve as components of a deliberately arranged environment. Contact with the unseen is mediated through setting, rhythm, and discipline.

The Seven Wonders extend this law from chamber to civilization. Great builders do not simply create impressive masses. They externalize number, planetary relation, initiatory memory, and cosmic proportion in stone, metal, and spatial order. Built form becomes a durable scripture.

So the chapter's implicit thesis is that ancient cultures mastered the art of making forms that continue to signify, act, or suggest long after their original keepers have vanished. Wonder is what remains when function is only partly remembered.
