Z1_B2 Text

This chapter is best read as an account of antiquity's power to preserve hidden knowledge in material form. Its wonders are not random marvels. They are survivals of sacred technique.

The perpetual lamps show one mode of preservation. Prepared matter shelters light so effectively that flame appears to outlast death itself. The image is both technical and symbolic: intelligence remains alight within darkness.

The oracles show a second mode. Sacred places, priestesses, fumes, trees, vessels, and rites are arranged to mediate utterance from another level of being. Prophecy is presented as managed contact rather than chaos.

The Seven Wonders gather these principles monumentally. Great works of architecture and sculpture become visible repositories of planetary, numerical, and initiatory doctrine. Their scale is secondary to their symbolic charge.

The chapter's enduring claim is that matter can become a faithful custodian of mystery. When living interpretation fades, what remains is wonder: the felt pressure of preserved function awaiting decipherment.
