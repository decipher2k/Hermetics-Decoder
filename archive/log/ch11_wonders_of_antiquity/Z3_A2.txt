Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter repeatedly unites preservation with controlled transformation.
- The sacred form does not merely hold content; it alters those who approach it.
- Wonder has initiatory force when encountered rightly.

Operative qualities
Transformative encounter, initiatory pressure, disciplined approach, and preservation-through-effect.

Reformulation

The chapter also becomes more active at this stage. Its forms do not simply endure; they act upon the one who approaches them. The noxious vapor throws the priestess into ecstasy. The cave of Trophonius deranges and then expels the consultant. The perpetual lamp astonishes those who break the tomb. Monumental works humble, orient, and educate through scale and pattern.

Preservation is therefore inseparable from effect. A sacred form truly survives when it can still reorder perception, disturb complacency, or awaken the sense of hidden law.

This gives wonder an initiatory dimension. The marvel is not merely observed; it places the observer under pressure to change posture. One must become more reverent, more interpretive, or more inwardly exact to receive what the form continues to offer.

Thus the chapter does not preserve antiquity as dead memory. It preserves encounters that still retain transformative charge.
