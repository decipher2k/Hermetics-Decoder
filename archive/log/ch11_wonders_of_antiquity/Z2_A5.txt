Central statements
- Planetary symbolism gives the wonders a unifying cosmological frame.
- The chapter reads grand artifacts as distributed embodiments of a larger cosmic body.
- Particular marvels become localizations of universal order.

Operative qualities
Cosmic distribution, planetary correspondence, localization, ordered plurality, and world-body symbolism.

Reformulation

The section on the Seven Wonders adds a stronger cosmological discipline to the chapter. These structures are not merely famous achievements. They are assigned to planets, linked to genii, and distributed as though the world itself were being articulated through selected nodal forms.

This transforms the chapter's scale. The lamp preserves one flame. The oracle releases one voice. But the wonders map the larger body of cosmic order across geography, architecture, and image. They are terrestrial stations of a planetary logic.

Each wonder thus becomes a localized condensation of a universal principle: solar glory, lunar generation, Jovian sovereignty, Saturnian illumination, Martian memorial force, Venusian fertility, Mercurial wisdom. Hall's comparative imagination turns the inhabited earth into a symbolic field.

The chapter therefore does more than celebrate ancient genius. It presents construction itself as cosmological inscription.
