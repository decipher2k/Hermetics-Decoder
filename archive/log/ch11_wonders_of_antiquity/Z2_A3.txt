Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter repeatedly stages entrances into altered states or hidden chambers.
- Thresholds are physical, psychological, and symbolic at once.
- Contact with the sacred requires passage through guarded transitions.

Operative qualities
Threshold-crossing, altered consciousness, guarded entry, ordeal, and transition.

Reformulation

A further line of convergence appears in the chapter's fascination with thresholds. Sealed tombs are opened. Vapors rise from fissures. Priestesses mount the tripod. Consultants descend into caves. Sacred groves are entered only after purifications. Monuments stand at charged sites. The wonder never occurs in flat homogeneity. It takes place at a boundary.

These thresholds are layered. Physically they involve chambers, vents, vessels, or architectural approaches. Psychologically they involve ecstasy, delirium, remembrance, terror, or reverent awe. Symbolically they mark passage between public life and a hidden order not ordinarily accessible.

The chapter thereby reinforces one of the book's central laws: revelation is mediated by initiation into a properly prepared interval. The profane world does not dissolve, but it is bracketed so that another intensity may be encountered.

Even the great monuments are threshold structures. They stand where a civilization touches its own highest image of order and seeks to make that contact durable.
