The chapter also sharpens a methodological principle already visible elsewhere: symbolic meaning arises from both physical property and formal use. Hardness, weight, transparency, brilliance, conductivity, rarity, and durability all matter. So do cutting, carving, placing, anointing, engraving, and wearing. Mineral symbolism is therefore inseparable from craft.

This is important because Hall's stone and gem correspondences are not abstract. They depend on the way material behaves and is ritually handled.
