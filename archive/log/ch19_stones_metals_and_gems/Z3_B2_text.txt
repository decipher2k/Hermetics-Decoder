This chapter presents the mineral world as the symbolic domain of permanence, support, and preserved power. Stones, metals, and gems are sacred because they endure, hold form, receive impression, and retain force. Hall begins with stone as the primordial footing of manifested life: the hard basis beneath body, temple, altar, and world. From archaic litholatry and poised monoliths to cubes, herms, and cornerstones, mineral substance becomes the emblem of all that supports and outlasts change.

The chapter then shows how truth is fixed into matter. Divine law appears on tablets; sacred boundaries are marked by pillars; the two columns of temple symbolism reveal the equilibrium of active and passive power; and the philosopher's stone names wisdom itself as the perfected agent of transmutation. The Holy Grail and related sacred jewels intensify the same doctrine from the side of receptivity: matter, when rightly prepared, can receive descending life and preserve it as vessel, heart, or cup.

Metals and gems extend this teaching into astral correspondence. Each substance is understood to partake of planetary virtues and therefore to serve as a regulator, transmitter, or condenser of celestial influence. Rings, talismans, seals, and magical ornaments unite material property, crafted form, inscription, and timing in order to make those correspondences operative. The chapter's deeper teaching is that matter need not be spiritually mute. In its most enduring and purified states it can become the stable body of law, memory, balance, and transmuting power.
