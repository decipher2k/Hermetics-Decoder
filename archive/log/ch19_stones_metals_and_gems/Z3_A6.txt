Final recomposition draft:

The chapter reads stones, metals, and gems as the enduring bodies of cosmic and spiritual power. Ancient peoples revered stone because it supports, preserves, and survives. From monoliths and altars to tablets, pillars, rings, and holy vessels, mineral matter becomes the medium in which law, covenant, memory, and sacred force are fixed against the dissolving power of time.

Within this field, the paired pillars clarify equilibrium, the philosopher's stone identifies wisdom as transmuting power, the Grail reveals receptivity to descending life, and metals and gems show how celestial influences may be condensed into material correspondences. Hardness, brilliance, rarity, form, inscription, and ritual use all contribute to a substance's symbolic force.

Mineral symbolism here is not primitive superstition. It is a disciplined account of how spiritual intention becomes durable and operative in matter.
