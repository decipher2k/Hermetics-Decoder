This chapter treats stones, metals, and gems as the most durable emblems of divine and cosmic power. The mineral world is revered because it preserves form, receives force, and supports life. Stone becomes the symbol of foundation, permanence, law, and primordial support; metals and jewels become the condensed bodies of planetary influences.

Monoliths, pillars, altars, and cubes all express the stability of truth and the necessity of fixed support for sacred order. The Tablets of the Law show revelation impressed into enduring medium, while the Holy Grail and sacred stones show matter as a vessel capable of containing descending life. The philosopher's stone deepens the doctrine by identifying wisdom itself as the power that transmutes what is base into what is precious.

The chapter culminates in talismanic mineralogy, where metals, gems, rings, and inscribed forms serve as deliberate instruments for receiving and directing celestial qualities. Matter is not dead here. It is the preserved body of living correspondences.
