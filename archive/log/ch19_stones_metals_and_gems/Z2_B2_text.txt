This chapter develops a symbolic mineralogy in which the hardest and most enduring substances of the world become the bearers of law, memory, and celestial force. Stone signifies primordial support and the stable body of truth. Pillars, herms, altars, and tablets all express this same doctrine in architectural, ritual, and legal forms. The sacred is repeatedly fixed into matter so that it may endure.

The chapter also makes mineral symbolism relational rather than inert. The paired pillars of temple tradition reveal balanced polarity; the Grail and holy jewel show receptivity to descending life; the philosopher's stone identifies wisdom as the transmuting principle that raises base substance toward preciousness. Metals and gems extend these ideas into astral correspondence, becoming substances that participate in and regulate planetary influence.

In the end, the chapter argues that matter is capable of preservation without spiritual emptiness. Properly prepared and rightly understood, it can carry covenant, equilibrium, and power.
