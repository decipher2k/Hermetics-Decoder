Chapter 19 treats stones, metals, and gems as the condensed and enduring bodies of cosmic forces. Mineral substance is not inert matter here. It is the fixed residue of celestial, elemental, and divine processes, and for that reason becomes especially fit to symbolize foundation, law, permanence, transmutation, and the containment of power.

The chapter opens with stone as the bodily analogue of spirit's supporting basis: bones, cliffs, foundations, pillars, tablets, and altars all carry the same logic of support. From there Hall moves through litholatry, sacred monoliths, rocking stones, cube symbolism, the philosopher's stone, the Mosaic tablets, and the Grail, showing again and again that hard substance becomes the emblem of truths meant to endure.

Metals and gems then extend this doctrine into astral correspondence. Planetary rays are believed to precipitate elemental and mineral bodies, so metals, jewels, and rings become regulators, transmitters, or condensers of celestial influence. The chapter's deeper claim is that matter can retain, transmit, and stabilize spiritual potency.
