Recomposed second-pass statement:

Hall uses the mineral world to explain how sacred order is fixed, preserved, and transmitted. Stone becomes the emblem of foundation and durable law; pillars and tablets mark threshold, polarity, and covenant; gems and holy vessels receive descending radiance; metals and rings bind celestial influences into operative form. The chapter teaches that matter can be sufficiently stable, pure, and prepared to hold spiritual intention without losing it.

Mineral symbolism is therefore the science of preserved correspondence.
