What must be avoided:

- Treating the chapter as a loose collection of mineral superstitions.
- Missing the continuity between stone, law, pillar, and equilibrium.
- Separating talismanic practice from the larger doctrine of cosmic correspondences.
- Reducing the philosopher's stone to chemical fantasy.

What must be preserved:

- Stone as enduring support.
- Mineral matter as receptive to celestial force.
- Inscription and law as forms of fixation.
- The union of cosmology, ritual, and craftsmanship in talismans and sacred objects.
