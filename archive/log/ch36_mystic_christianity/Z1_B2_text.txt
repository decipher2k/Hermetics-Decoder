This chapter presents Christianity as a mystery doctrine veiled by historical uncertainty, miracle accretion, and dogmatic simplification. Hall distinguishes between Jesus as historical initiate and Christos as the indwelling divine principle to which the Gospel story points. He links the hidden teaching to Essenes, Templars, eastern contacts, and secret custodians of sacred history. The central claim is that salvation means reunion with the divine Self within man. The Christian drama is therefore initiatory: death, resurrection, mediation, and ascent are inward processes before they are public beliefs.
