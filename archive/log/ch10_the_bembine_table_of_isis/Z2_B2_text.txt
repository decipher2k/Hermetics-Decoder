The chapter can now be read as an account of the Bembine Table as preserved operative cosmology. Its importance lies in the fact that form retains function even after the original ceremonial life has receded from view.

This is why the chapter treats symbolic exactitude so seriously. Ritual efficacy depends on analogical alignment: garments, gestures, emblems, chants, placements, and figures must correspond rightly if higher powers are to be invoked or mirrored.

The Table's internal order is emanative. Triads, septenaries, panels, borders, gates, and the central throne count and distribute the descent of hidden plenitude into visible multiplicity. Number is therefore structural, not decorative.

The enthroned center gives the entire object coherence. From concealed crown to surrounding hierarchies, the Table stages centered diffusion. Its parts are not fragments; they are radiations.

The chapter also clarifies why the Table can be read as cosmos, body, altar, initiation, and alchemy at once. These are lawful transpositions of one design across multiple scales.

The lasting claim of the chapter is that symbolic objects become intelligible only when activated structurally. The Bembine Table is a preserved system of relations, not a heap of enigmatic images.
