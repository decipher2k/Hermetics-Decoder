This chapter presents the Bembine Table as a compressed analogical cosmology. It is not important merely as an old object, but as a ritual and doctrinal instrument in which theology, world-order, symbolic language, and initiatory method are concentrated.

For that reason the chapter repeatedly moves past antiquarian description. Material, dimensions, ownership, and scholarly controversy matter only insofar as they frame a more important question: what kind of symbolic intelligence made such an object, and how was it used?

Kircher's theurgic account answers by making ritual precision central. Symbol, garment, gesture, chant, and arrangement form an exact chain through which human action seeks analogical accord with divine pattern. The Table is therefore part of an operative ceremonial field.

Its structure is read as emanative. Triads, panels, crowns, central figure, surrounding hierarchies, and lower distributions all show unity unfolding into structured multiplicity. The enthroned transmitting principle at the center makes the whole arrangement coherent by mediating between hidden source and distributed worlds.

The chapter also insists that the Table supports multiple but related readings. It may be read as cosmology, anthropology, initiation-map, alchemical schema, temple plan, or generative mystery. These are coherent because the same analogical order governs them all.

The chapter's lasting claim is that hidden wisdom can be built into symbolic objects so densely that they function at once as altar, diagram, teaching-device, and key. The Bembine Table is one of the book's clearest examples of concentrated esoteric form.
