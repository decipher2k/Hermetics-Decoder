Central statements
- The chapter presents the human body as the oldest and most universal symbolic textbook.
- Macrocosm and microcosm are treated as mutually intelligible through analogy.
- Theology, cosmology, and initiation are grounded in the structure of man.

Operative qualities
Correspondence, embodied knowledge, nested scale, symbolic anatomy, and revelatory analogy.

Reformulation

This chapter presents the human body as the master symbol through which ancient wisdom approached the structure of reality. Rather than attempt to grasp the immeasurable directly, early philosophers turned to man as the condensed and studyable form of universal law. What exists in the vastness outside also exists, by analogy and proportion, within.

This gives the chapter its governing claim: man is the little world, the universe the great one. Macrocosm and microcosm are not merely similar by poetic comparison. They are structurally continuous. The same powers, laws, and orders appear at both scales.

The body therefore becomes more than flesh. It is theological diagram, cosmological map, and initiatory manual. Organs, centers, limbs, proportions, garments, and functions all disclose larger realities.

The chapter should thus be read as one of the book's clearest efforts to relocate doctrine inside embodied structure.
