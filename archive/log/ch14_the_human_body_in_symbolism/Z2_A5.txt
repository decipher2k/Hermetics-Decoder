Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter repeatedly treats clothing and ornament as symbolic of degrees of manifestation.
- Form itself is vesture.
- Virtue and attainment are the lasting adornments of the inner man.

Operative qualities
Vesture, graduated manifestation, earned radiance, and moral embodiment.

Reformulation

The doctrine of garments sharpens the chapter's symbolic richness. Clothing is equivalent to form. Bodies, robes, colors, and ornaments all express degrees of manifestation and inward condition.

This means that virtue is not an abstract possession. It clothes the being. Purity, sacrifice, integrity, and other qualities appear as modes of visible or symbolic apparel.

The chapter therefore reads external beauty and deformity as codified expressions of deeper realities, not as mere decoration.

Anatomy and vesture join to show how inward attainment becomes outwardly legible.
