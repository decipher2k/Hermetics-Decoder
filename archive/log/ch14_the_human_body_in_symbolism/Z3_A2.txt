Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter unites concealment and disclosure through bodily signs.
- Myth, allegory, anatomy, and ritual all hide practical knowledge in plain sight.
- Symbolic embodiment protects transformative doctrine.

Operative qualities
Protective disclosure, encoded practicality, visible concealment, and initiatory reserve.

Reformulation

The chapter also becomes one of the clearest examples of protective disclosure in the whole book. Myths of creation, sacred figures, anatomical correspondences, colored garments, and ritual chambers all preserve practical knowledge about transformation while concealing it from unqualified use.

This is why bodily symbolism proves so fertile. It is visible to everyone and intelligible to few. The same body can be ordinary flesh to one observer and a complete theological map to another.

The chapter therefore shows how esoteric doctrine survives by embedding itself in forms too familiar to seem dangerous.

Embodiment becomes the perfect veil precisely because it is universal.
