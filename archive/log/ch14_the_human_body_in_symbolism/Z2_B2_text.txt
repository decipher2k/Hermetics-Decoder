The chapter can now be read as an account of the body as inward measure. Human form is used to map the cosmos because its proportions, centers, limbs, and features disclose larger laws of relation.

This mapping demands subtlety. Right and left, light and darkness, heart and brain, inner and outer all require corrected symbolic reading. Superficial moral oppositions distort what the body is meant to teach.

The chapter also extends embodiment beyond the individual. Communities, religions, races, and worlds are treated as composite organisms, so the logic of the microcosm scales upward.

Garments, colors, and bodily forms then reveal degrees of manifestation, while the doctrine of descent and awakening shows why the body matters soteriologically: it is the site where higher life dreams in matter and may be consciously restored to source.

The chapter's lasting claim is that symbolic anatomy is a path of interpretation, measure, and living reversal.
