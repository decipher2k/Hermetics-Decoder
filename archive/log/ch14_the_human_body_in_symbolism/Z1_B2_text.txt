Z1_B2 Text

This chapter is best read as an account of the human body as the master symbol of sacred philosophy. The body condenses within itself the same laws, powers, and orders that appear in the wider universe.

For that reason, ancient theologians and initiates treated man as the proper measure of cosmology. Macrocosm and microcosm are structurally continuous.

The chapter also insists that symbols must not harden into idols. Once the key is lost, sacred figures and even the body itself are misread as outer terminals rather than reminders of deeper realities.

Its teaching on heart, brain, and generative force then organizes the body as a triadic field of spiritual, mental, and formative power. Temples and rites are built on this same pattern.

The chapter's lasting claim is that the body is not only a sign to be read, but the very place where regeneration and reunion with source may occur.
