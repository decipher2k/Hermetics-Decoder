Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter repeatedly distinguishes symbol from idol.
- The body and sacred image are reminders of hidden realities, not final objects of worship.
- Loss of interpretive key turns living symbol into dead effigy.

Operative qualities
Reminder, veiling, misreading, symbolic fall, and recovery of meaning.

Reformulation

Another major line in the chapter is the distinction between symbol and fixation. The ancient manikin in the sanctuary, the temple image, the body itself, and later religious figures were meant to indicate invisible powers. They were not supposed to arrest attention at the level of outer form.

The chapter then narrates a fall in interpretation. Once the key is lost, symbol hardens into idol. The mysterious human figure once used as a map of universal power becomes worshiped as though it were itself the divinity.

This gives the chapter historical as well as metaphysical force. It is not only about what the body means, but about how meaning is forgotten when sign and source are confused.

The body becomes instructive again only when restored to its role as living emblem rather than terminal object.
