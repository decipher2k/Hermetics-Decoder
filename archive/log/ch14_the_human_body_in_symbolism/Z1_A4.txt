Central statements
- The chapter organizes the body through centers and vertical hierarchy.
- Heart, brain, and generative system are read as linked powers.
- Spiritual, mental, and vital functions mirror cosmic structure.

Operative qualities
Centering, vertical order, mediation, triadic embodiment, and graded power.

Reformulation

One of the chapter's strongest structures is its teaching on the three great centers. The heart, brain, and generative system are not merely organs. They are loci through which spirit, intellect, and formative life are staged in the body.

Their relations are deliberately subtle. The heart is said to be most inward and spiritually prior, the brain a mediating and dignified link, and the generative system the lower but physically potent source of embodied production. These centers define the human being as a vertically ordered field.

The chapter makes clear that life-quality depends on which center predominates. Materialism, mentalism, and initiation are distinguished by different internal governments.

The body thus becomes a triadic cosmogram in which anthropology and theology meet.
