Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter uses the body as a universal measuring rule.
- Limbs, organs, fingers, sides, and features generate a symbolic arithmetic of form.
- Embodiment yields proportion and taxonomy.

Operative qualities
Measure, proportional mapping, morphological order, and embodied taxonomy.

Reformulation

The chapter also becomes clearer as a science of measure. The body's five extremities, the four limbs under one head, the ten fingers, the phalanges, the vertical halves, the facial trinity, and the multiple correspondences to elements, planets, and zodiacal divisions all show that human embodiment supplies a grammar of proportion.

This measuring function is not incidental. It allows the ancients to organize cosmology through a directly available form. The body becomes a standard by which larger structures can be patterned and interpreted.

The result is a symbolic arithmetic of anatomy. Number, proportion, and bodily differentiation cooperate.

The chapter therefore extends the previous mathematical materials into living morphology.
