Extraction

Central statements
- Left and right, light and darkness, offensive and defensive are treated as symbolic polarities.
- The chapter reinterprets common moral oppositions as structural distinctions.
- Darkness is frequently anterior rather than evil.

Operative qualities
Polarity, reversal, hidden priority, protective asymmetry, and corrected symbolism.

Reformulation

The section on the body's halves introduces a corrective polarity. Right and left, light and darkness, offensive and defensive, masculine and feminine are all given symbolic meaning; yet the chapter repeatedly warns against moral simplification. Darkness can signify anterior hiddenness rather than evil. The heart's left-sidedness becomes especially important in this reversal.

This makes the chapter critical as well as descriptive. It is undoing degraded symbolic habits that confuse subtle distinctions with moral condemnation.

The body is therefore not only a set of correspondences but a school in how to read polarities without collapsing them into crude binaries.

Such correction is essential if symbolic anatomy is to remain faithful to deeper doctrine.
