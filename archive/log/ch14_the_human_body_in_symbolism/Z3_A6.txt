Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter ultimately treats initiation as conscious reunion during life.
- Death is no longer the only path to rejoining the higher nature.
- The body can become sanctuary rather than prison.

Operative qualities
Conscious reunion, living initiation, sanctified embodiment, and transcendence without flight.

Reformulation

At its strongest, the chapter makes a decisive regenerative claim: what death would ordinarily accomplish unconsciously, initiation can accomplish consciously during life. The sleeping portion of the higher nature may awaken within embodiment and be reunited with its source without bodily destruction.

This transforms the whole meaning of the body. It ceases to be only tomb, prison, or veil and becomes sanctuary, threshold, and site of realized return.

The chapter therefore culminates not in theory but in promise. Embodiment can be redeemed from below by conscious illumination.

The body becomes the place where transcendence is achieved without abandonment of life.
