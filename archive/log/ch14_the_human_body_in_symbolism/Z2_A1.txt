Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter increasingly reads as a doctrine of interiority.
- What is most real in man is most hidden and most central.
- Outer anatomy points inward toward deeper orders of life.

Operative qualities
Interiority, hidden centrality, inward descent, and layered self-disclosure.

Reformulation

On second pass, the chapter's center shifts toward interiority. The body matters because it points inward, not because it exhausts meaning outwardly. The more hidden the principle, the more central and causative it becomes.

This clarifies why the heart can outrank the visibly higher brain, why garments can signify form, and why the body itself may be temple, prison, robe, or sanctuary according to the degree of awakening. All outer arrangements refer inward.

The chapter therefore teaches an inward hermeneutic. Anatomy is not an end-point but an invitation to pass from surface structure into subtler orders of life.

The body becomes readable only when the interpreter moves from visible configuration to invisible causation.
