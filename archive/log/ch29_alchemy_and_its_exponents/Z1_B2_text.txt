This chapter defends alchemy by restoring seriousness to its exponents. Hall argues that an art affirmed across centuries by philosophers, physicians, princes, and disciplined experimenters cannot be dismissed without intellectual laziness. He therefore turns to exemplary lives. Paracelsus represents the fusion of Hermetic science, medical reform, and uncompromising independence. Lully, Flammel, and Bernard of Treviso represent pilgrimage, hidden books, revelatory encounters, patience, and the long discipline of failed attempts finally answered. The chapter's deeper point is that alchemy is known through transformed and tested persons as much as through propositions.
