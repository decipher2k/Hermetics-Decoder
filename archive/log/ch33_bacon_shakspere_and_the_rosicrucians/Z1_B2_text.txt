This chapter treats the Bacon-Shakspere controversy as a problem of symbolic transmission rather than celebrity attribution. Hall argues that the plays and related printed works likely carried a level of Rosicrucian and Masonic doctrine too intricate to be explained by the traditional biography of Shakespeare alone. Bacon becomes significant not merely as an alternative author but as a master of concealment, someone capable of using drama, print design, ciphers, and editorial devices to preserve secret teachings. The chapter's deeper claim is that literature can function as the outer body of an invisible fraternity.
