This chapter presents the Baconian controversy as evidence for a larger program of hidden preservation. Hall reads plays, folios, title pages, ornaments, watermarks, page errors, acrostics, and signatures as coordinated signs that literature and print design were used to conceal Rosicrucian and Masonic teachings in plain sight. Bacon matters because he embodies the range of knowledge and the cryptographic discipline required for such a project. The question is therefore not merely who wrote the plays, but how a secret fraternity might preserve ancient wisdom through public books while leaving keys for future discoverers.
