Extraction

Central statements
- The three murderers are symbolic agencies rather than merely villains.
- They represent recurrent obstructing powers at many scales.
- Gate symbolism organizes the descent into material interruption.

Operative qualities
Triplicity of obstruction, threshold assault, distortion, and recurrent opposition.

Reformulation

The three ruffians are among the chapter's most important symbolic devices. They are not merely criminal personalities. They represent the repeated triads by which higher work is obstructed: ignorance, superstition, and fear; state, church, and mob; birth, growth, and decay; seasonal and zodiacal agencies.

Their placement at the gates matters as much as their violence. The assault occurs at thresholds, where sacred power seeks passage into embodiment and action. The gates become points of crisis.

This transforms the murder into a law of interruption. Whenever higher building enters the lower world, opposing agencies strike it through distortion, coercion, and misreading.

The chapter thereby gives obstruction a patterned form. Evil is not random; it attacks where transmission must pass.
