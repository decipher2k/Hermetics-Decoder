Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter increasingly reads as a theory of transmission under rupture.
- What is most precious is concealed in form, grave, sign, and ritual.
- Loss tests whether continuity can survive without direct possession.

Operative qualities
Transmission under rupture, preservative concealment, tested continuity, and ritual memory.

Reformulation

On second pass, the chapter's center shifts toward transmission under rupture. CHiram's death is devastating precisely because he carries the master's word, the higher key, and the ordering principle of the work. Yet these do not vanish completely. They pass into concealment.

The legend therefore asks how truth survives when its living bearer is lost. The answer is through sign, ritual, memory, proportion, and the structure already impressed into the temple itself.

This makes burial preservative as well as tragic. The grave does not only hide. It holds in trust.

The chapter thus becomes a meditation on how wisdom remains recoverable after historical breakage.
