Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter's many identifications are structured by analogy, not confusion.
- Osiris, Christ, Bacon, de Molay, the sun, and spirit-fire are parallel enactments.
- One pattern is transposed across traditions and scales.

Operative qualities
Analogical transposition, patterned recurrence, cross-traditional continuity, and synthetic readability.

Reformulation

The variety of identifications in the chapter can now be read properly as analogical transpositions. Hall is not collapsing all figures into sameness, but using each to illuminate the same regenerative sequence under different conditions.

This is why the chapter can move from Egypt to Christianity, from alchemy to astronomy, from civic history to inner anatomy without losing coherence. Each domain offers another enactment of the same pattern.

The legend therefore serves as a comparative instrument. It teaches how one law of descent, interruption, burial, and raising may be recognized across many symbolic worlds.

This synthetic readability is one of the chapter's greatest strengths.
