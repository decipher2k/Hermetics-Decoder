Z1_B2 Text

This chapter is best read as an account of the Hiramic legend as a regeneration myth. CHiram is the builder-spirit whose labor erects sacred form and whose death, burial, and raising encode the recovery of higher power through initiation.

The temple is central because it represents form made worthy of indwelling truth. Its silent and exact construction images harmony already achieved before visible assembly.

CHiram himself is repeatedly universalized, becoming a type of cosmic martyrdom, archetypal man, and the principle of truth and beauty working in the world.

The three murderers symbolize recurrent obstructing powers that strike at the gates where spirit passes into manifestation. Yet burial does not end the work. The hidden sign remains, and the fallen builder is raised.

The chapter's enduring claim is that what is slain in history, culture, and the individual may be restored if the buried word and the beautifying principle are recovered.
