Extraction

Central statements
- Burial and raising are essential, not secondary, to the legend.
- Loss becomes the condition for recovered power.
- The evergreen mark and lion's grip signify continuity through concealment.

Operative qualities
Concealed continuity, death-to-restoration, remembered life, and initiatory raising.

Reformulation

The chapter does not stop with murder. Burial and raising complete the pattern. CHiram is hidden in the earth, marked by an evergreen sprig, sought by ordered parties, and finally raised by a superior power. Continuity survives in concealment.

This means that loss is not the negation of the work but part of its transformation. The buried word, the hidden body, and the preserved sign all prepare a recovered relation that is stronger because it passes through death.

The acacia and lion's grip therefore signify more than memorial. They indicate the persistence of living force through interruption.

The chapter thus makes initiation inseparable from rediscovery of what seemed destroyed.
