Central statements
- The chapter resolves into CHiram as the buried creative ideal in form and history.
- Buildings, bodies, institutions, and myths all contain the concealed builder.
- Interpretation becomes resurrection.

Operative qualities
Buried ideal, concealed presence, interpretive resurrection, and immanent truth.

Reformulation

On the third pass, the chapter's deepest unity appears in the claim that CHiram is the buried creative ideal within forms. Temples, rituals, political orders, myths, atoms, and bodies all conceal a truth they can no longer openly display.

This makes interpretation itself a mode of resurrection. To understand symbolic form correctly is to release the principle hidden within it.

The chapter therefore reaches beyond Masonic legend into a general theory of culture. Every worthy form is the tomb of an eternal verity awaiting recovery.

CHiram becomes the name of concealed presence in the world.
