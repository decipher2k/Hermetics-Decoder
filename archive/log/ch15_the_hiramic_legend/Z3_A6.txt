Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter opposes despair by insisting that burial is not final.
- Repeated slaying does not cancel the eventual triumph of truth.
- The pattern of raising gives history a redemptive horizon.

Operative qualities
Redemptive persistence, undefeatability of truth, hope through pattern, and eventual vindication.

Reformulation

At its strongest, the chapter argues against despair. Truth, virtue, and beauty may be calumniated, persecuted, or buried again and again, but the pattern itself promises eventual raising. Evil may interrupt; it cannot complete the story.

This is why the chapter repeatedly returns to resurrection, rediscovery, and restored mastery. History remains tragic, but not closed.

The Hiramic pattern therefore gives hope form. It does not deny rupture; it embeds rupture inside a larger law of recovery.

The chapter becomes a discipline of redemptive historical vision.
