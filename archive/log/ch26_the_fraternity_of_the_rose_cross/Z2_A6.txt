The link to alchemy now sharpens the chapter's inner unity. The philosopher's stone, elixir, dew, transmutation, and secret fire recur because the Rosicrucian project is fundamentally one of human refinement. Metallurgical language is both metaphor and technique, but Hall's emphasis falls on the person as laboratory. Ignorance, selfishness, and coarseness are the true base metals.

This makes the brotherhood less a society of collectors of secret facts than a fellowship of practitioners of interior chemistry. Healing, wisdom, and secrecy all follow from that. Those who have not transformed themselves cannot safely wield the science.

The chapter thus belongs closely to Hall's moralization of occultism: hidden power is legitimate only when governed by inward regeneration.
