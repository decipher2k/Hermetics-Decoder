The ethical core of the chapter also deserves emphasis. Again and again Rosicrucian access is tied to purity, humility, healing intent, and worthy selection. The unclean heart cannot force entry. This places the fraternity firmly within Hall's larger moral framework: higher knowledge protects itself against selfish motive by becoming inaccessible to it.

The anonymity of the brethren is therefore not only strategic but ethical. It denies vanity the reward of occult prominence. Hidden service, free healing, and refusal of external credit become marks of authenticity. The Brotherhood is less a club of special privileges than a discipline of self-effacing wisdom.

This helps explain why the chapter remains sympathetic even at its most skeptical. The ideal it describes is spiritually coherent.
