The chapter's treatment of Masonry, Bacon, Andreæ, St.-Germain, and later orders now falls into place. These figures matter as possible nodes in a chain of transmission, but Hall never allows genealogical curiosity to displace symbolic insight. The real issue is whether they carried, distorted, or concealed parts of the same current. Rosicrucian influence is read in continuities of emblem, reforming aspiration, Hermetic vocabulary, and initiatory style.

This permits a careful middle position. One need not prove a monolithic invisible empire directing Europe to recognize that esoteric ideas can move through semi-hidden networks of persons, texts, and societies. The Brotherhood may be less centralized than legendary imagination suggests and more real than skeptical dismissal allows.

Its history is thus best read as patterned diffusion under protective obscurity.
