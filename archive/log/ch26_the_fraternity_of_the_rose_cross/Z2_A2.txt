Another refinement is the fraternity's relation to reform. The Fama does not merely introduce a hidden order; it frames that order as a response to corruption in religion, science, and learning. The Rosicrucian mission is restorative. It seeks to recover a wisdom broad enough to heal, illuminate, and reorganize knowledge under divine orientation.

This aligns the Brotherhood with other reforming currents in the book, but with one important difference: it works primarily through anonymity. Hall emphasizes that the brethren take no outward dignity, often wear no distinctive garb, and may move invisibly within ordinary trades and professions. Reform here comes from concealed centers rather than public spectacle.

The idea is philosophically important. It suggests that decisive cultural influence may proceed from persons whose authority is symbolically or spiritually operative before it is socially acknowledged.
