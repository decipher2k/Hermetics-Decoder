The C.R.C. legend now reads as an initiation myth of transmission. The youthful seeker leaves inherited confinement, studies with distant sages, returns with a book, experiences rejection by the learned, gathers a few faithful companions, establishes a hidden house, and leaves behind a preserved body within a secret vault that opens only after a full cycle. Every element expresses the passage of wisdom from hidden source to hidden guardianship rather than to mass reception.

The tale is therefore structurally true even if historically embroidered. It describes how esoteric reform works: through pilgrimage, translation, disappointment with public authority, small circles of trust, coded labor, and posthumous discovery. Hall reads the legend as a vehicle for the biography of wisdom itself.

This makes the chapter less about one man than about a recurring pattern of initiatory custodianship.
