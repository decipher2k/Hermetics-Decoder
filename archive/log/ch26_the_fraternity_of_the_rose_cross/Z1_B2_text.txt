This chapter treats the Rosicrucian Brotherhood as one of the most elusive and suggestive enigmas in the history of esotericism. Hall organizes the inquiry around competing explanations: the fraternity may have existed historically as described in its manifestoes, may have been a real order masked by allegorical founding myths, may have begun as literary invention, or may have been a transcendental society whose true operations were never meant to be publicly verified. Rather than deciding hastily among these options, the chapter uses them to show how difficult it is to approach a tradition that defines itself through concealment.

The legend of C.R.C. provides the chapter's imaginative center. His travels, initiation among Eastern sages, healing work, secret house, chosen brethren, and preserved tomb compose a myth of hidden reforming wisdom. Whether literal or symbolic, the story presents the Rosicrucian ideal as a fraternity of men dedicated to healing, reformation, secrecy, and the guarding of a sacred science from the unworthy. The hidden vault with its seven-sided form, its books and altar, and the delayed revelation of its contents dramatizes one of the chapter's main laws: truth is buried until those capable of receiving it appear.

The chapter's deeper stability lies in its symbols. The rose and the cross summarize the mystery of spiritual unfoldment through embodied limitation, and Rosicrucian alchemy is treated less as metallurgy than as the regeneration of man. Hall therefore suggests that the true Rosicrucian current may persist even where documentation fails: wherever wisdom is concealed in symbols, transmitted by worthy selection, and directed toward the transmutation of the human being, the Brotherhood's essential work continues.
