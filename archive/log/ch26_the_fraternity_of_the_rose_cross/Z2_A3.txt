This pass also strengthens the distinction between the fraternity as institution and the fraternity as attainment. Hartmann's definition of the Rosicrucian, repeated by Hall, makes clear that the name belongs properly to one who has undergone spiritual awakening and grasped the practical significance of the rose and the cross. One cannot be made a Rosicrucian by label alone.

That distinction recasts the entire chapter. If the true fraternity consists of inwardly transformed persons, then outward organizations may at best approximate it. They can serve as vessels, schools, or masks, but not as guarantees. This explains why later societies may bear the name while missing the essence.

The chapter therefore privileges interior realization over formal membership. The brotherhood is real wherever the work of regeneration is real.
