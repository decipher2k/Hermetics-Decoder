This chapter presents Rosicrucianism as a problem in hidden authenticity. Hall surveys the principal theories concerning the Brotherhood of the Rose Cross and shows that each preserves part of the truth. The fraternity may have existed historically, may have clothed itself in allegorical biography, may have been amplified by literary invention, or may have operated on levels not fully accessible to ordinary historical verification. The important point is that the Rosicrucian tradition defines itself through concealment, and therefore cannot be judged solely by the standards used for public institutions.

The C.R.C. narrative gives the chapter its symbolic body. The travels to Eastern wisdom centers, the healing ministry, the House of the Holy Spirit, the chosen brethren, and the discovery of the seven-sided vault present a fraternity devoted to the preservation and guarded release of sacred science. Hall is less concerned to prove each episode than to show what sort of tradition this legend describes: one that reforms by secrecy, heals without self-display, and reveals itself only to worthy seekers. The tomb episode in particular symbolizes the delayed unveiling of doctrine kept hidden until the proper cycle and the proper eyes arrive.

The rose-cross emblem gathers the chapter's deepest meaning. Rosicrucianism is finally the science of regeneration by which the soul unfolds through and beyond the cross of embodied existence. Alchemy, Qabbalah, mysticism, and fraternal symbolism meet in that one image. For Hall, the Brotherhood remains plausible and important because its essence is not exhausted by documents. Wherever symbols are used to protect transformative knowledge, wherever wisdom is transmitted through selective initiation, and wherever man himself becomes the subject of the alchemical work, the Rosicrucian current is still alive.
