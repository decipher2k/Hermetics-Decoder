The chapter's connection to Freemasonry now becomes clearer. Hall does not claim a simplistic identity between the two, but he repeatedly treats Rosicrucianism as a likely conduit by which older Hermetic and symbolic materials entered later fraternal forms. The importance of this claim lies less in institutional genealogy than in the survival of a symbolic method across orders.

Rosicrucianism here stands for a mode of preserving wisdom through symbols whose keys are withheld. Masonry may preserve the outward emblems while losing or fragmenting the interpretive core. This possibility gives the Brotherhood a special place in Hall's narrative: it is the hidden keeper of the meanings later systems only partially display.

The result is a chapter about guarded transmission. Wisdom survives not by universal publication alone but by selective embodiment in persons, emblems, rites, and myths capable of crossing centuries.
