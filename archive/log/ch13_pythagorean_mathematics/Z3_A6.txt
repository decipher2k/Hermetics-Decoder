Central statements
- The chapter opposes both reductionism and arbitrary mysticism.
- Its rigor lies in holding formal exactness and symbolic depth together.
- True numerological understanding is disciplined, not improvisational.

Operative qualities
Balanced rigor, anti-reductionism, anti-arbitrariness, and disciplined symbolics.

Reformulation

At its strongest, the chapter resists two failures at once. On one side lies reductionism, which treats number as quantity alone. On the other lies arbitrary mysticism, which assigns meanings without law. Hall's reconstruction of Pythagorean mathematics rejects both.

The science of number remains exact, classified, and formal. Yet that formal precision opens into ethics, cosmology, symbolism, and theology. Depth does not dissolve rigor.

This balance makes the chapter especially important in the book's overall method. It demonstrates how symbolic interpretation can be disciplined rather than vague.

The chapter can thus be read as a defense of principled esoteric intelligence.
