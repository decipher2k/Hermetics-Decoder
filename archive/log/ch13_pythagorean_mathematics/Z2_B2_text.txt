The chapter can now be read as an account of number as relational ontology. Arithmetic precedes the other sciences because it shows how connection, proportion, and dependence make any form of ordered knowledge possible.

Its fragments are handled with caution because the original key was guarded and largely lost. Yet the remnants still preserve a clear direction: number is lawful, qualitative, and generative.

The many classifications of number are therefore not incidental. They form a typology of being, distinguishing different modes of relation, balance, sufficiency, divisibility, and excess.

The table of ten numbers then stages this field developmentally, as a sequence through which unity unfolds into a complete world of powers and returns to intelligible completion.

The chapter's lasting claim is that mathematical process is also metaphysical process: a movement of articulation, differentiation, and recollection.
