Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter relates numerical order to ethics and virtue.
- Perfect, deficient, and superabundant numbers become analogues for moral proportion.
- Mathematics is inseparable from the question of right measure.

Operative qualities
Measure, virtue, balance, excess and defect, and ethical analogy.

Reformulation

The section on perfect, deficient, and superabundant numbers reveals that the chapter is not interested in mathematics for its own sake. Numerical relations are repeatedly taken as images of ethical realities. Virtue occupies the mean between excess and defect just as perfect number stands between deficiency and superabundance.

This is a crucial move in the chapter. Arithmetic does not end in abstract structure. It becomes moral instruction because right proportion governs both number and life.

The rarity and ordered generation of perfect numbers then become especially significant. Goodness is neither common nor disorderly. It appears through exact relation.

The chapter therefore uses number to teach not only how things are arranged, but how measure should govern character and action.
