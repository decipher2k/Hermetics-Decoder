Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter resolves into number as the bridge between hidden principle and visible order.
- Arithmetic makes metaphysics teachable through exact relation.
- Numerical thought translates mystery into form.

Operative qualities
Bridging, teachable metaphysics, exact translation, and visible articulation.

Reformulation

On the third pass, the chapter's deepest function appears: number is the bridge by which hidden principle becomes teachable without ceasing to be profound. Arithmetic does not merely count what is already visible. It translates unseen order into formal relation.

This makes the chapter a close companion to the preceding Pythagoras chapter. What Pythagoras embodied educationally, mathematics now expresses structurally. Mystery becomes exact without becoming flat.

The long treatment of derivations, classes, and numerical names therefore serves a single purpose: to show that metaphysical order can be learned through disciplined relation.

The chapter becomes an instructional threshold where esoteric doctrine passes into intelligible method.
