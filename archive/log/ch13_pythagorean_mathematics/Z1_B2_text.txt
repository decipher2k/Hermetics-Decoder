Z1_B2 Text

This chapter is best read as an account of number as the first intelligible structure of reality. Pythagorean mathematics is presented not as calculation alone but as a sacred science of principles.

Its method is reductive in the strict sense. Words return to original tongues, higher numbers to foundational digits, and derived sciences to arithmetic. Surface multiplicity becomes readable only by tracing it back to source.

The monad stands at the center of this system. From unity proceed the many, yet the many remain structurally linked to their origin. Numerical classes then express different modes of relation, from polarity and divisibility to equilibrium and completion.

The chapter also binds mathematics to ethics. Perfect, deficient, and superabundant numbers image virtue's measure between excess and defect.

Its culminating table of the ten numbers shows arithmetic becoming symbolic cosmology. Each numeral names a mode of being, and together they form a complete grammar of the world.
