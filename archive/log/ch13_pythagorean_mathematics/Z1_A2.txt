Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter insists that numerical meaning depends on original language, original form, and correct derivation.
- Reduction to first principles is a recurring method throughout the chapter.
- Surface form must be led back to structural source.

Operative qualities
Reduction, exact derivation, return to source, formal precision, and hidden equivalence.

Reformulation

One of the chapter's first stabilizing movements is reduction back to origin. Words must be returned to Hebrew or Greek. Higher numbers must be reduced to foundational digits. Derived sciences must be led back to arithmetic. Each of these procedures expresses one law: meaning becomes clear only when surface complexity is traced back to first structure.

This makes precision essential. Numerical interpretation is not free invention. It depends on correct language, proper form, and disciplined derivation. Hall is careful to separate older principial systems from later speculative distortions that merely borrow Pythagorean prestige.

The chapter therefore combines esotericism with rigor. It offers secrecy, but not vagueness. The hidden is lawful, and the lawful can be approached only by careful reductions rather than fanciful association.

This methodological severity gives the long numerical catalogue its coherence. Every classification serves the return from multiplicity to ordered source.
