Extraction

Central statements
- Numerical classes become modes of world-reading.
- Odd/even, perfect/deficient, and composite/incomposite map styles of being.
- Mathematics becomes ontology by classification.

Operative qualities
Typology, ontological reading, differentiation, and patterned character.

Reformulation

The chapter's many classifications now appear less technical and more ontological. By sorting numbers into odd and even, composite and incomposite, evenly-even and oddly-odd, perfect and deficient, Pythagorean mathematics develops a typology of relation.

Each class becomes a way of reading reality. Stability, divisibility, incompleteness, sufficiency, excess, and hidden balance are no longer abstract labels but styles of being.

This explains why the chapter can so easily move from numerical operations to deities, virtues, cosmology, and symbolic names. Classification is carrying the burden of ontology.

The chapter therefore teaches that to classify well is already to interpret the world's inner character.
