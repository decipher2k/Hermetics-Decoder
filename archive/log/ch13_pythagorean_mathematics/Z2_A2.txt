Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter repeatedly joins secrecy with formal exactness.
- The lost key does not imply arbitrariness; it increases the need for careful reconstruction.
- Fragments retain lawful direction.

Operative qualities
Guarded knowledge, lawful remnant, disciplined reconstruction, and exact interpretation.

Reformulation

The opening emphasis on lost oral transmission matters more on this pass. The key to the science is missing, but the fragments are not therefore meaningless. They still point in a lawful direction. Hall's repeated caution against overstatement is part of the chapter's rigor.

Secrecy here functions as a test of interpretive discipline. One must work from remnants without pretending to possess what has vanished. That posture keeps the chapter from dissolving into fantasy.

This also explains the careful attention to ancient alphabets, proper derivation, and the rejection of careless modern numerology. Hidden doctrine remains exact even when partially lost.

The chapter thus models a restrained esoteric method: recover what can be defended, preserve the direction of the whole, and refuse decorative excess.
