Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter increasingly reads as a science of relation rather than of quantity.
- Numbers matter because they show how parts belong within wholes.
- Arithmetic becomes the discipline of intelligible connection.

Operative qualities
Relation, belonging, articulation, connective order, and structural seeing.

Reformulation

On second pass, the chapter's center shifts from number as source to number as relation. The reason arithmetic precedes the other sciences is that it reveals how parts connect, proportion, divide, and gather. Geometry needs relation to become shape. Music needs relation to become harmony. Astronomy needs relation to become ordered motion.

This means the chapter is not really about isolated integers. It is about intelligible belonging. A number is meaningful because it locates a thing within a field of ratios, dependencies, and structural possibilities.

The many examples of divisibility, halving, compositeness, and proportion all train the mind to see this connective order.

The chapter therefore becomes a discipline of relational perception: to think numerically is to see how things cohere.
