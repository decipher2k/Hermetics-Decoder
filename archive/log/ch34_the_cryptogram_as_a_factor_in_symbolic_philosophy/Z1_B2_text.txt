This chapter presents cryptography as a necessary companion to symbolism. Hall argues that whenever sacred or dangerous knowledge must be preserved without open disclosure, ciphers become indispensable. He extends the meaning of cryptogram far beyond secret alphabets to include page design, watermarks, acrostics, numbering, allegory, pictures, music, and every deliberate arrangement through which a hidden message may survive. The chapter's deeper point is that human cryptography imitates Nature herself, whose visible forms are characters of an invisible alphabet. To learn decipherment is therefore to train the very faculties required for symbolic philosophy.
