This chapter develops cryptography into a general theory of hidden communication. Hall shows that ciphers are not confined to letter substitution but can inhabit numbers, diagrams, initials, images, page structures, parables, and even music. The acroamatic form is especially powerful because allegory can preserve many levels of meaning across time and translation. In this way cryptography becomes the technical side of esotericism: a method for releasing truth proportionally, protecting it from the profane while rewarding the disciplined reader with deeper access.
