Reformulation

The chapter resolves into an argument about memory-bearing form. Myths endure because they do not only recount events; they store processes. Atlantis stores loss of center and scattered continuity. The dying-god myths store descent, grief, ordeal, and restoration. These are reusable patterns for interpreting both history and self.

That is why the chapter remains active rather than antiquarian.
