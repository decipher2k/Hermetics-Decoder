Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter's center is no longer Atlantis alone but the afterlife of a lost center.
- What survives catastrophe is symbolic and ritual architecture.

Operative qualities
Afterlife of origin, symbolic survival, ritual continuity, and distributed memory.

Reformulation

The chapter increasingly centers on the afterlife of a lost center. Atlantis matters not only because it vanished, but because its disappearance forces wisdom into displaced forms: myths, sacred emblems, priesthoods, rites of mourning, resurrection dramas, and recollections of civilizing visitors.

What is lost as geography survives as symbolic architecture. The chapter therefore reads catastrophe as a mode of redistribution.

This logic governs both halves of the section. Atlantean memory diffuses outward into symbols and institutions. The dying-god traditions preserve the same law inwardly by teaching that what dies or descends can become the site of regeneration.
