Extraction

Central statements
- The dying-god myths are read anthropologically and initiatically.
- The soul descends, loses its adornments, suffers imprisonment, and may reascend.

Operative qualities
Soul-descent, stripping, captivity, ascent, and recovered adornment.

Reformulation

The chapter repeatedly interprets dying-god rites as allegories of the soul. Ishtar's descent through seven gates becomes the spirit entering material embodiment by losing its higher adornments. The return upward signifies release through the water of life, or secret doctrine. Atys, Adonis, and other savior-gods likewise dramatize the loss of divine standing and the possibility of recovery.

In this frame, ritual mourning is not only grief for a deity. It is the recognition of the human condition: fallen into time, severed from source, and awaiting rebirth through knowledge and transformation.

The chapter's myths therefore operate as condensed maps of incarnation and redemption.
