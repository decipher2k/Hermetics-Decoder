Convergence check
The chapter is moving toward a strong dual reading: Atlantis serves as memory of primordial order lost through corruption and catastrophe, while the dying-god traditions preserve the same structure in ritual form as descent, suffering, concealment, and restoration.

Reformulation

Read together, Atlantis and the dying-god myths form one doctrine expressed on two scales. Atlantis is the fall of a world. The dying god is the fall of consciousness within the world. In both cases, something ordered descends into disruption, yet fragments of the original law survive and can guide regeneration.

The chapter's symbols, myths, and comparative leaps all reinforce this continuity. Lost continents, sacred mountains, sea-born teachers, serpents, crosses, buried gods, ritual lamentations, sacramental meals, and resurrections all become variations on one memory-pattern: wisdom descends, is obscured, and must be recovered through symbolic participation.
