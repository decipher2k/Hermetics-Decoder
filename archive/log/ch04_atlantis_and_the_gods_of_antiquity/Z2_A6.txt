Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter links ancient myth to later Christianity through structural continuity.
- Christianity is presented as inheriting, not abolishing, the dying-god formula.

Operative qualities
Structural continuity, inheritance, martyr pattern, and salvific drama.

Reformulation

The chapter repeatedly points toward Christianity as heir to the dying-god structure. The crucifixion and resurrection do not abolish the older ritual grammar; they inherit and intensify it. Hall's concern is not reduction but continuity of form: divine suffering, sacrificial death, mourning, descent, and restored life as the key to redemption.

This widens the chapter from antiquarian study into a theory of enduring religious structure.
