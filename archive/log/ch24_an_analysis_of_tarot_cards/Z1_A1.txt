Chapter 24 presents the Tarot as a portable architecture of wisdom disguised as a game. Hall treats it as a symbolic book whose images, numbers, sequences, and suit structures encode the same universal principles already traced through Qabbalah, alchemy, astrology, initiation, and sacred anthropology. The chapter therefore does not merely catalog cards; it argues that a complete symbolic language was hidden in plain sight by being entrusted to amusement, gambling, and popular usage.

Its governing premise is that the cards are simultaneously discrete principles and relational grammar. Each trump is a complete hieroglyph in itself, yet each also gains further meaning by place, sequence, and interaction with the others. The Tarot is thus a philosophic alphabet whose symbols can be read independently, combinatorially, and structurally.

The chapter then demonstrates this claim by reading the major trumps as stages, principles, and powers of the cosmic and human drama. The cards become signs of emanation, choice, sacrifice, reversal, death, balance, ascent, judgment, and completion. The minor arcana and even ordinary playing cards then reveal how the symbolic code survives in reduced and popularized form.
