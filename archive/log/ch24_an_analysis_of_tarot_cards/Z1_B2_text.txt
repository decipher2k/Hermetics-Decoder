This chapter presents the Tarot as a symbolic book whose wisdom survived by taking the outward form of a game. Hall argues that the deck is not merely a collection of curious pictures but a philosophic alphabet in which each card is a complete hieroglyph and each also occupies a meaningful place within a larger order. The Tarot therefore operates both as a series of separate teachings and as a continuous sequence of transformation.

The major trumps are read as stages, powers, and events within the human and cosmic process: concealment, authority, polarity, choice, victory, justice, withdrawal, reversal, death, balance, temptation, rupture, hope, uncertainty, illumination, awakening, and consummation. The deck becomes a compressed map of initiation. The minor arcana then extend the symbolism into the elements, seasons, social functions, Qabbalistic worlds, and even the ordinary structure of the year.

The chapter's deeper teaching is that symbolic knowledge can be preserved by passing into apparently trivial forms. What the world handles as play may still carry the architecture of wisdom.
