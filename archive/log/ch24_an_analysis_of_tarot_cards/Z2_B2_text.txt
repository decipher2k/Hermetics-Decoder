This chapter develops the Tarot as a deliberately veiled sequence of symbolic instruction. Hall treats the deck as a public object carrying secret order: its images are accessible to all, but their true content emerges only when they are read numerically, structurally, and philosophically. The cards therefore belong to the same symbolic universe as Qabbalah, alchemy, Masonry, and sacred anthropology.

The major trumps describe a path. The seeker enters in ignorance or innocence, passes through power, law, polarity, choice, motion, justice, solitude, reversal, mortality, balance, temptation, destruction, uncertainty, illumination, awakening, and totality. The zero card, whose place is disputed, embodies the mystery of beginning outside the counted order while still making the sequence possible.

The minor arcana and later playing cards preserve the same wisdom in abbreviated form, distributing it into elemental suits, courtly hierarchies, social classes, seasonal divisions, and calendrical patterns. The chapter argues that symbolic play can become the safest archive of dangerous knowledge.
