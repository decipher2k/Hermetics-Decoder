This chapter presents the Tarot as a concealed book of universal knowledge preserved in the outward form of cards for play. Hall treats the deck as a symbolic language in which image, number, sequence, and emblematic detail are inseparable. Each card is a complete hieroglyph representing a principle, power, law, or stage of process, yet the full doctrine emerges only when the cards are read in relation to one another as a philosophic alphabet and an ordered path of transformation.

The chapter's strongest emphasis falls on the major trumps, which become stations in the drama of consciousness and cosmos. Through them Hall traces entrance into experience, the handling of power, the concealment of wisdom, authority, polarity, choice, movement, justice, withdrawal, reversal, mortality, balance, temptation, destruction, uncertainty, illumination, awakening, and consummation. The disputed zero card concentrates the deepest mystery of the deck because it stands at the threshold of enumeration: both outside the series and the hidden condition that makes the series traversable. In this way the Tarot becomes a visual account of how the unformed enters form and how the seeker moves through the successive veils of manifested life.

The minor arcana, ordinary playing cards, and even chess reveal the survival of the same symbolic order in reduced and popularized forms. The suits mirror elements, classes, seasons, and worlds; the court cards preserve zodiacal and hierarchical meanings; the yearly calendar is silently encoded in the deck's structure. The chapter's enduring teaching is that wisdom can survive by disguise. What appears to be amusement may still carry a complete initiatory memory for those able to read through number, image, and sequence toward the law concealed within them.
