Final recomposition draft:

The chapter reads the Tarot as a concealed philosophical text preserved under the outward form of cards for play. Hall argues that the deck is a symbolic language whose images, numbers, and order encode a complete doctrine of the world and the soul. Each card is an independent hieroglyph, yet each also participates in a larger sequence of transformation, so that the deck becomes both alphabet and drama, inventory and path.

Within this symbolic book, the major trumps trace the movement of consciousness through power, polarity, choice, justice, withdrawal, reversal, death, balance, temptation, catastrophe, hope, uncertainty, illumination, awakening, and world-completion. The disputed zero card concentrates the deepest mystery by standing both before and outside the numbered order, marking the threshold where hidden totality enters manifest sequence. The minor arcana extend the same architecture into the four elements, the social orders, the calendar, and the Qabbalistic worlds, while ordinary playing cards and even chess preserve diminished but still legible fragments of the same older design.

The chapter's enduring teaching is that symbolic knowledge can survive by migration into image, game, and habit. The Tarot is a portable structure of memory in which universal process remains readable to those trained to see through the mask of amusement.
