First transmutation:

The chapter converts medicine from intervention upon inert matter into cooperation with a living field. The physician is not mechanic first, but interpreter of disturbed relations. Disease appears when the channels through which life descends into embodiment become misaligned, obstructed, contaminated, depleted, or karmically burdened. Hence cure must often occur by restoring pattern before tissue.

This is why the chapter tolerates such a broad therapeutic repertoire. Sound, color, herbs, amulets, prayer, diet, and practical treatment are not competing schools here. They are distinct ways of influencing the vehicles through which life operates. The key question is always correspondence: what medium can receive, divert, counterbalance, or replenish the disturbed force.
