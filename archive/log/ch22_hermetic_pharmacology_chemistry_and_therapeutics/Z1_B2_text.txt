This chapter reunites medicine with the sacred sciences by presenting healing as the restoration of harmony across visible and invisible levels of being. The art of healing, Hall argues, originally belonged to the temple, where philosophy, religion, and science were understood as one discipline rather than separated arts. Disease cannot therefore be understood solely as a bodily accident; it arises first in subtler disorders of life force, mind, etheric form, karmic compensation, or astral imbalance.

Paracelsus stands at the center of the chapter because he recovers this integral vision. Through concepts such as archæus and mumia, he explains how vital force enters bodies, how invisible disturbance produces physical ailment, and how therapeutic action must work through appropriate media to restore proper flow. Herbs, talismans, prayer, sound, diet, color, astrology, and practical treatment are thus different but related means of influencing the same layered organism.

The chapter's enduring teaching is that true medicine interprets causes and assists Nature, rather than attacking symptoms blindly. Healing is the reordering of relation.
