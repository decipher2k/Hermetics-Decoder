The seven causes and seven cures now stand out as the chapter's core architecture. Disease is shown as multi-causal: demonic or parasitic influence, disharmony between subtle and material nature, mental poisoning, karmic compensation, celestial pressure, misuse of faculty, and impurity or obstruction. The corresponding cures are equally plural: command, vibration, talismans, herbs, prayer, regimen, and practical intervention.

This confirms that the chapter resists one-cause, one-cure thinking. It is structurally anti-reductionist.
