This chapter develops a philosophy of medicine in which healing is possible only when the physician understands the layered constitution of man and the mediating channels through which life operates. Disease is not reduced to surface symptom or mechanism. It may arise from disordered thought, astral poisoning, karmic necessity, celestial pressure, misused function, impurity, or broken coordination between subtle and physical bodies.

Paracelsus serves as the chapter's chief witness because he restores a medical vision broad enough to include observation, herbs, talismans, prayer, sound, astrology, regimen, and practical intervention without collapsing them into confusion. Concepts such as archæus and mumia explain how force moves, how maladies are carried, and how remedies can redirect the diseased current toward purification or release.

The chapter insists that medicine is most true when it becomes both symbolic and exact: reading hidden causes while helping Nature re-establish order in the visible body.
