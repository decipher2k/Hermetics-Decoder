Chapter 22 re-sutures healing to the wider sacred sciences. Its central claim is that medicine was originally inseparable from philosophy, religion, cosmology, and initiation, and that modern therapeutic reductionism is a symptom of the same fragmentation that has damaged every other field of knowledge in the book.

Hermetic medicine begins from a different image of man. The human being is not merely a material organism but a layered constitution whose visible body is shaped, nourished, and disturbed by invisible forces, vehicles, and relations. Disease therefore cannot be understood only at the level of tissue and mechanism. It must be traced to disorder in the astral, vital, mental, moral, karmic, celestial, or etheric dimensions that precede bodily manifestation.

Paracelsus becomes the chapter's chief exemplar because he reunites observation, experiment, symbolism, astrology, pharmacology, and metaphysical anthropology. The chapter uses him to argue that true medicine guides Nature by understanding hidden causes rather than violently suppressing visible effects.
