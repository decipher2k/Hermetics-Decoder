Final recomposition draft:

The chapter reads ceremonial magic as a symbolic science whose legitimacy depends entirely upon the moral and spiritual state of the operator. Circles, seals, pentagrams, vestments, planetary hours, divine names, and invocations are all treated as real mediating forms. Yet Hall's real concern is not their mechanics but their misuse. The moment occult knowledge is pursued for selfish gain, domination, curiosity, or fear-driven self-preservation, the entire symbolic order reverses.

In that reversal priesthood becomes manipulation, invocation becomes coercion, pact becomes bondage, and sacred emblems become inverted signatures of descent. The black magician imagines himself commander, yet every bargain with lower force increases dependency and terror. No external protection can finally secure the one who has not mastered himself.

Ceremonial magic here becomes the book's sharpest lesson that symbolic power without ethical transformation is spiritually fatal.
