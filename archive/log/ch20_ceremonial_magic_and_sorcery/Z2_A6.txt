Hall uses ceremonial magic to expose the moral conditions of occult practice. Ritual forms do possess mediating force, but they become destructive when used coercively rather than sacramentally. Black magic is the inversion of lawful relation: a turning of symbols, offices, and powers toward appetite, domination, secrecy, and survival. The pact with spirits dramatizes the inevitable reversal by which the would-be master becomes a dependent servant of the very force he sought to exploit.

The chapter's deeper teaching is that no one can safely govern the invisible who has not first governed himself.
