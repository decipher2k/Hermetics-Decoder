Recomposition sketch:

Hall presents ceremonial magic as an ancient science of invoking and directing invisible powers by means of symbols, names, circles, instruments, and timed ritual acts. Yet the chapter is fundamentally cautionary. The same science that may theoretically serve worthy ends becomes black magic when occult force is perverted toward selfish desire, domination, curiosity, or the prolongation of personal power. Corrupt Atlantean and Egyptian priestcraft are treated as large-scale examples of this degeneration, in which sacred symbols are distorted, the Mysteries mutilated, and the masses reduced to superstition.

The chapter culminates in the logic of the pact and the inversion of symbols, especially the pentagram. Its central law is that power sought without self-mastery reverses the seeker into the servant of lower forces.
