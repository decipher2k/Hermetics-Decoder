This chapter develops a critique of ceremonial magic by distinguishing lawful symbolic mediation from coercive occult exploitation. Hall does not deny that circles, seals, vestments, planetary timings, and invocations can mediate contact with invisible forces. Rather, he insists that these forms become perilous when used by an ungoverned will seeking knowledge, power, curiosity, domination, or survival.

Black magic is therefore presented as an inversion, not a separate wisdom. The same symbolic field is turned downward. Priesthood becomes spiritual tyranny, sacred signs become instruments of manipulation, and invocation becomes bargaining with lower forces. The pact with the demon compresses the whole logic: what begins as command ends as dependency, fear, and eventual bondage.

The chapter's essential judgment is ethical. No ritual safeguard can compensate for a disordered soul. Without inner mastery, the operator is already aligned with the powers he pretends to control.
