The pact now emerges as the chapter's strongest teaching-image. It compresses the whole logic of misused occultism: the desire for power leads one to bargain with what cannot be safely governed, and every attempt to secure advantage multiplies future dependency. The magician thinks in terms of exchange, but the exchange is structurally unequal because the lower force always works to convert temporary mastery into ultimate claim.

This makes fear of death central. The sorcerer seeks extension because he knows the direction of the bond he has created. Power without purification becomes dread.
