First transmutation:

This chapter studies the point at which knowledge of correspondences becomes dangerous. The same symbolic world that can educate, heal, and align becomes destructive when approached as a technology for forcing the invisible to gratify the ego. Ritual is not condemned in itself; what is condemned is the will that uses sacred form as leverage rather than as discipline.

For that reason the chapter treats black magic as a moral deformation before it is a technical one. It begins in selfishness, hardens into pact, and ends in fear, dependency, vampirism, and spiritual inversion. The sorcerer wants command, but each gain binds him more tightly to what he imagines he commands. False transcendence becomes intensified servitude.
