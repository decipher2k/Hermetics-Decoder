Second pass clarifies that the chapter is built on a distinction between mediation and coercion. Ritual, in its lawful form, mediates relation between worlds through order, purity, and alignment. Sorcery seeks to coerce relation for gain. The outer apparatus may look similar in both cases, but the governing intention changes the whole metaphysical meaning.

This is why Hall spends so much time on circles, swords, seals, pentacles, contracts, and formulas without endorsing them. The forms matter, but motive and character decide whether they remain sacramental or become predatory.
