Reformulation

The Preface ultimately declares an ethic of transmission. Ancient wisdom has not only been forgotten; it has often been weakened by the forms through which it survived. This book is an attempt to answer that weakening by giving the material a vessel more proportionate to its dignity. The care spent on beauty, order, and material form is therefore part of the book's truthfulness.

The visible writing lasted for years, but it was preceded by quieter years of gathering, selecting, and organizing. What now appears as a volume once existed as dispersed sources waiting to be brought into relation. Footnotes were removed to preserve the flow of understanding. References were brought inward. The bibliography and index were retained so that the reader could continue the work rather than simply consume it.

I do not claim final certainty or absolute originality. The material itself forbids such postures. Ancient testimony reaches us in fragments, and faithful handling requires caution. Some passages here may challenge familiar habits of thought, yet they are offered with restraint and with an effort to preserve the living impulse of the original sources. Whatever remains mistaken belongs to me.

This work does not impose a personal creed, nor does it erase genuine divergence among traditions in the name of easy unity.

It does, however, oppose the narrowing assumptions of modern materialism. Its aim is to widen the reader's horizon of life and thought. Because the material is extensive, no arrangement can be perfect, but relation has been sought over fragmentation.

Such reading demands more than literal intelligence. It asks for a reader capable of symbolic depth, patient resonance, and disciplined receptivity.

The book further bears the mark of collective support. Libraries preserved sources. Translators reopened them. Editors and craftsmen gave the whole a coherent and worthy body. Subscribers trusted the work before completion.

Gratitude is therefore not incidental to this Preface. It is part of the author's philosophy of how serious work becomes possible.

My hope is that the same movement may continue in the reader: that what was gathered, ordered, and shaped here may become widened perception there.
