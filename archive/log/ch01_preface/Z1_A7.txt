Central statements

- Research, translation, and production are presented as sacred labor of mediation.

Reformulation

This work begins from a simple but demanding conviction: truths of lasting depth should not be handed on in forms that diminish them. Many books have written about the concealed philosophies of antiquity, yet the enduring life within them has too often been poorly clothed. I have tried to fashion a book worthy of the sages whose thought it gathers, trusting that the effort to unite beauty with truth will bear fruit in the reader's mind.

Although the manuscript itself was written over more than two years beginning in 1926, its visible form rests on much earlier labor. Sources had been gathered since 1921, and the structure of the book gradually clarified. Footnotes were removed so that the argument could move without constant interruption, while references were placed within the text where they could serve living understanding. The bibliography remains as a guide for further study, and the index was built to make complex material easier to approach.

I do not offer these pages as final or infallible. The remnants of ancient teaching do not justify dogmatic certainty, and certainty without proportion has damaged philosophy more than it has strengthened it. Some passages here may seem bold, but they are not the result of careless speculation. I have tried to preserve the spirit of the original materials without imprisoning them in dead literalism. If errors persist, they are my responsibility.

I also offer no private creed. I have not turned old writings into proofs for prior convictions, nor have I tried to smooth over the real tensions among the world's religio-philosophic systems.

The orientation of this book opposes prevailing modern assumptions because it takes seriously subjects often dismissed before they are understood. Its aim is to open the reader to a way of thinking and living that exceeds materialist reduction. Since the material is extensive and difficult, it cannot be perfectly arranged, yet it has been ordered as carefully as possible.

Language, even in its richness, fails at times before subtle metaphysical matters. A reader who approaches this work well must therefore bring an inward responsiveness capable of hearing more than the bare words say.

This book also stands on the generosity of many others. Libraries, scholars, translators, editors, designers, printers, and friends all shared in its becoming. Their help is part of the work itself, not an incidental addition.

Translation formed the heaviest portion of the research. Multiple languages had to be rendered faithfully before the book could take coherent shape, and generous collaborators made that possible.

The material book likewise required careful hands. Editorial order, illustration, typography, fitting of text to space, and quality of printing all had to serve the same end: that the vessel not betray the content.

The remarkable advance subscription of the book revealed another truth: the work came into the world through confidence before completion.

For that reason I remain indebted to those whose faith financed what they had not yet seen.

If these pages enlarge the reader's life even modestly, they will have fulfilled their purpose. In writing them I discovered truths, learned patience, submitted to order, encountered craft, and gained friends. Those discoveries are now entrusted to whoever opens the book.
