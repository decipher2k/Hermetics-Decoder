Extraction

Central statements
- The Preface condenses the whole work into an ethic of preserving depth.
- The author accepts limit while sustaining confidence in meaning.
- Reading becomes an exercise in attunement.

Operative qualities
Preservation of depth, bounded confidence, and attunement.

Reformulation

At its most concentrated, the Preface says this: if wisdom is deep, it must be preserved in a form that does not flatten it. That is why this book has been made with unusual care. The aim is not display, but fidelity.

The finished volume stands on years of prior labor. Research, collection, translation, and ordering made the later writing possible. Footnotes were omitted to protect continuity. References were integrated into the prose. The bibliography and index remain to support further movement through the material.

I do not claim a certainty the sources cannot support. Ancient testimony is partial, and responsible interpretation must remain aware of that boundary. Some claims here may be demanding, but they have not been advanced without care. I have tried to stay near the inward meaning of the material. Any failure is mine.

No personal doctrine governs the use of these sources. No artificial harmony has been created by suppressing real differences.

The work nevertheless challenges the narrowing habits of modern thought by reopening neglected dimensions of life and understanding.

To meet those dimensions, the reader must cultivate attunement. Literal reading alone will not do.

This book also acknowledges the network of people and institutions that made it possible.

Its gratitude is therefore a recognition of reality, not an ornament.

If it leaves the reader more capable of sensing depth within form, then the Preface has already done its work.
