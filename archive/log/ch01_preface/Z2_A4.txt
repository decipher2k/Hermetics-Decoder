Central statements
- The Preface is not preliminary ornament; it defines the ethic of the whole work.
- The book presents truth as something requiring form, patience, and restraint.
- It rejects modern haste and interpretive violence.
- It calls for a reader who can sustain subtlety.

Operative qualities
Ethical framing, patience, restraint, anti-haste, and sustained subtlety.

Reformulation

What this Preface tries to establish is not merely gratitude or background, but the ethic by which the whole book should be read. Ancient wisdom has too often been delivered in forms that flatten its power. This volume attempts to offer a more adequate vehicle, one in which care for appearance is inseparable from care for truth.

The book was written over more than two years, but writing was only the visible phase of a much longer discipline. Research, collection, comparison, and planning preceded the manuscript. Footnotes were removed so the flow of understanding would not be repeatedly broken. References were incorporated into the body of the work, the bibliography was preserved for later students, and the index was built to guide readers through complexity.

I do not claim a final or unassailable authority. The surviving materials of antiquity do not permit that kind of certainty. Some statements may sound strange to modern ears, yet they have not been advanced irresponsibly. I have tried to remain close to the inward direction of the sources and to avoid both careless invention and dead literalism. Any errors are my own.

This work is not the instrument of a personal doctrine, nor is it an attempt to force all traditions into synthetic agreement.

Its orientation stands against modern haste and reduction. It addresses matters often set aside as impossible, irrelevant, or absurd by those who do not first learn how to read them. The book therefore seeks to make a broader mode of understanding imaginable again.

Because the subjects are subtle, language alone is not enough. The reader must bring patience, receptivity, and a capacity for meanings that unfold rather than announce themselves all at once.

The volume further depends on a network of support. Libraries, translators, editors, artists, printers, friends, and advisors all share in its existence.

Without translation the book would have remained divided from essential parts of its material. Without editorial and material care it would have failed to embody what it meant to preserve.

Without subscribers it might not have appeared at all.

To each of these forms of support I owe sincere thanks.

My hope is that the reader may receive not only information from this work but a changed relation to the material it gathers. That hope justified the years of labor.
