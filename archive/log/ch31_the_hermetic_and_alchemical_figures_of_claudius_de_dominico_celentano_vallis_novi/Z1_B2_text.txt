This chapter presents the illustrated manuscript of Claudius de Dominico Celentano Vallis Novi as a living specimen of Hermetic pedagogy. Hall interprets its bottles, colors, birds, trees, queens, saints, and vessels as a continuous symbolic rehearsal of the Great Work. The sequence from quicksilver through raven-black corruption, whitening, flowering, and red consummation is treated not as mere chemical fancy but as a coded account of human regeneration. The manuscript's style is intentionally strange because ridicule protects its secrets. Its deeper claim is that the Work must be contemplated through metamorphic symbols until the reader learns to recognize one process under many images.
