Reformulation

Philosophy here becomes a veil-lifting discipline. It tests visible claims, sorts them, and tries to see through them toward the source that gives them form. Its value lies in this movement from outer multiplicity toward inner ground.

Its many definitions can all be understood as attempts to describe that same movement. Whether one speaks of causes, principles, reason, truth, selfhood, or unity, philosophy remains a labor of penetration.

Its branches map the field through which penetration proceeds: being, logic, ethics, inward life, knowledge, and beauty.

The chapter argues that this labor was once elevated and transformative, but later became fragmented when schools forgot their own deeper origin.

The survey of schools supports that claim. Each takes one access route and refines it: element, number, permanence, hidden structure, soul-work, renunciation, pleasure, skepticism, law, tranquility, system, experience, intuition, will, utility, evolution. The result is a rich but derivative diversity.

Visual inserts make visible what abstraction alone might conceal. Structured multiplicity, guarded thresholds, unified plurality, and images of enclosed becoming all suggest that visible forms can hold formative experiences and teachings more stably than plain exposition.

The chapter then identifies the older formative experience from which philosophy later derived: the sacred disciplines of the Mysteries, nourished by older Eastern centers of learning and only partially expressed in Greek schools.

Once this is seen, public philosophical history becomes secondary articulation of a more primary hidden life.

Secrecy explains the broken record. What was most potent was most carefully veiled.

Symbolism preserved continuity across those breaks. It allowed formative insight to survive where institutions, languages, and explicit doctrines might fail.

Ancient monuments, myths, measures, rites, and sacred images therefore become points of re-entry into depth.

The Introduction ends by handing the reader that point of re-entry. Learn to read symbolically, and later chapters become recoveries rather than summaries.
