Reformulation

The Introduction now holds together as an integrated method. Philosophy is presented as the movement from appearance toward source. The schools are presented as partial and visible branches of that movement. The hidden root of those branches is located in sacred initiation. Symbolic forms are presented as the medium through which that concealed continuity survives.

This method clarifies why philosophy has so many definitions. Each definition marks a different effort to speak the same ascent from multiplicity toward governing truth.

It also clarifies why philosophy has so many branches. Being, logic, ethics, inward life, knowledge, and beauty are differentiated approaches to the same deep labor.

It clarifies the history of schools as well. From earliest cosmologies through mathematical, rational, atomistic, moral, skeptical, stoic, theological, empirical, idealist, intuitive, evolutionary, and pragmatic traditions, philosophy repeatedly branches without escaping its primary questions.

The chapter's images, captions, and visionary episodes are integral because they model how visible form can bear more than exposition alone can stabilize.

The genealogical turn then places Greek philosophy within a larger sacred inheritance shaped by older Eastern centers and preserved in the Mysteries.

Secrecy explains the thinness of direct evidence. Symbolism explains the survival of hidden meaning despite that thinness.

The reader is therefore not merely informed by the Introduction. The reader is initiated into a way of seeing: public doctrine and symbolic residue must be read together if concealed continuity is to appear.

With that method in hand, the rest of the book becomes readable as an archive of enduring but veiled wisdom.
