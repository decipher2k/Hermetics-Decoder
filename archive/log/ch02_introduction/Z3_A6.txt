Reformulation

The stable core of this Introduction can now be stated plainly. Philosophy is the effort to move from visible order to invisible source. The history of philosophies records many partial articulations of that effort. The oldest root of those articulations lies in sacred institutions of hidden wisdom. Symbolic form preserves their continuity across ages of loss.

The many definitions of philosophy all describe the first movement. The familiar branches of philosophy all specialize it. The long sequence of schools all diversify it.

The ancient esteem for philosophy reflects its power to elevate thought. Modern fragmentation reflects what happens when elevation gives way to rivalry among partial claims.

The chapter's survey of schools, from earliest cosmologies through modern national traditions, therefore serves a single interpretive end: to show branching diversity without surrendering hidden continuity.

Its figures and poetic passages support the same end by demonstrating that visible form often preserves layered relation more faithfully than exposition alone.

The chapter's genealogical turn then identifies the deeper root in older Eastern learning and in the Mysteries, where doctrine and transformation were inseparable.

Secrecy and symbolic preservation become the necessary conditions of endurance.

The reader is thereby given an interpretive frame for the whole book.

Without that frame, later chapters remain scattered materials. With it, they become branches of a concealed but recoverable wisdom.
