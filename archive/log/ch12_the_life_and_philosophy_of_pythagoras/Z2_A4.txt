Extraction

Central statements
- Microcosm and macrocosm relations are central to the chapter.
- Man and universe mirror one another because both bear the same image.
- Cosmology becomes anthropology.

Operative qualities
Mirroring, correspondence, shared image, inward cosmos, and reciprocal understanding.

Reformulation

Another strong line of convergence is the chapter's insistence that man and universe are made in the same image. The Grand Man and the little universe interpenetrate. To know one rightly is to advance toward knowledge of the other.

This principle explains why so many domains belong together in the chapter. Astronomy, geometry, ethics, bodily care, healing, transmigration, and symbolic figures all become intelligible as different scales of one order.

The Pythagorean worldview therefore prevents both abstraction and isolation. The human being is not detached from the cosmos but is its local recapitulation.

The chapter's anthropology is thus cosmological through and through: self-knowledge is impossible apart from structure of the whole.
