Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter links self-government to cosmic intelligibility.
- One who cannot govern appetite, speech, and excess cannot understand order rightly.
- Ethics is epistemic, not merely moralistic.

Operative qualities
Self-rule, restraint, clarity, moral cognition, and ordered freedom.

Reformulation

The chapter's ethical emphasis grows sharper when read epistemically. Moderation, silence, non-anarchy, truthful prayer, vegetarian caution, and bodily care are not simply moral rules imposed from outside. They are conditions for clear perception.

If excess disorders the soul, it also disorders knowledge. A clouded life produces a clouded intellect. This is why judges should avoid meat before judgment, why speech should be governed, and why indolence should not be aided.

Pythagoras' ethic is therefore ordered freedom rather than repression. The aim is to bring the lower powers into measure so that intelligence may recognize the higher.

The chapter thus ties wisdom to self-government with unusual firmness.
