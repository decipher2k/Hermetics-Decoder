Central statements
- The chapter binds metaphysics to practical disciplines of diet, healing, friendship, and speech.
- Philosophy is shown as embodied regimen.
- The soul is clarified through daily forms of moderation and harmony.

Operative qualities
Embodiment, moderation, healing, friendship, and ethical practice.

Reformulation

Pythagoras' teaching is not left at the level of speculative cosmology. The chapter repeatedly descends into habits: diet, prayer, restraint of speech, friendship, healing by music and plants, refusal of excess, and disciplined regard for the body as a sacred dwelling.

These details are not quaint eccentricities. They show that philosophy must inhabit conduct if it is to be real. Harmony is not merely heard in the heavens; it must be cultivated in appetite, language, relation, and bodily care.

Friendship receives special dignity because it models concord without coercion. It is one of the clearest human analogues of cosmic order.

The chapter therefore presents Pythagoreanism as an art of lived alignment, where daily regimen becomes the vehicle of metaphysical participation.
