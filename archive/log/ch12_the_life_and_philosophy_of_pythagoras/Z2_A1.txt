Extraction

Central statements
- The chapter increasingly reads as a pedagogy of attunement.
- Pythagorean disciplines tune the human being to hidden order.
- Knowledge is resonance before it is proposition.

Operative qualities
Attunement, resonance, inward tuning, educational calibration, and consonance.

Reformulation

On second pass, the chapter's center shifts from integration in general to attunement in particular. Everything Pythagoras teaches aims to tune the human being: number orders thought, music harmonizes affection, astronomy expands vision, silence refines speech, and regimen steadies desire.

This makes knowledge fundamentally resonant. One does not master truth from outside; one becomes proportionate enough to sound with it. The Pythagorean school is therefore less a repository of information than an instrument for calibration.

Even the chapter's biographical material now serves this idea. Travels through many mystery centers suggest a gathering of tonalities, later resolved into one disciplined mode of life.

Pythagoras emerges as the teacher of concord, one who forms souls capable of recognizing the lawfulness already present in cosmos, friendship, number, and virtue.
