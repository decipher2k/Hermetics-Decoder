Central statements
- The chapter unites authority and humility in a productive tension.
- Pythagoras is revered almost as divine, yet philosophy begins in seeking.
- The teacher's stature serves formation, not domination.

Operative qualities
Reverent authority, humility, exemplary presence, disciplined discipleship, and non-possessive guidance.

Reformulation

Another mature feature of the chapter is the tension between Pythagoras' exalted status and his definition of philosophy as seeking. He is treated as Master, wonder-worker, revealer, and almost divine presence; yet his own title points away from possession toward love of wisdom.

This tension is productive. Authority here exists to awaken aspiration and discipline, not to cancel inquiry. The teacher is a living measure of what ordered life can become.

The disciples' reverence, the sacredness of his name, and the loyalty surviving his death all show authority as formative magnetism. It organizes a community of pursuit.

The chapter thereby defends a model of spiritual-intellectual leadership in which greatness intensifies the search rather than closing it.
