Reformulation

On the third pass, Pythagoras appears as one of the book's great translators. He takes the diffuse inheritance of mystery cultures and renders it into method: degrees, studies, symbols, aphorisms, regimen, and community. Hidden wisdom becomes teachable without becoming trivial.

This makes the chapter pivotal. What earlier chapters showed in temple, myth, and sacred artifact now becomes educational architecture. The mysteries are not abandoned; they are reorganized into a school of disciplined intelligibility.

Pythagoras therefore stands at the passage from archaic custody to philosophical articulation. He shows how esoteric knowledge can become systematic while remaining initiatory.

The chapter's real biography is thus the biography of transmission itself.
