Extraction

Central statements
- Symbols and aphorisms function as compressed training devices.
- The Y, the tetractys, and the aphorisms encode exercises in perception and choice.
- Pythagorean teaching is deliberately condensed.

Operative qualities
Compression, mnemonic discipline, symbolic exercise, moral choice, and layered instruction.

Reformulation

The chapter also clarifies the pedagogical role of symbols. The forked Y, the tetractys, the sacred name, and the aphorisms are not ornamental emblems. They are condensed exercises designed to reshape perception and conduct.

The Y dramatizes the forking of the ways, making moral choice visible in one simple sign. The tetractys gathers cosmological order into a numerical figure fit for contemplation, oath, and recollection. The aphorisms force the disciple to unpack instruction inwardly rather than consume it passively.

This compressed style belongs to guarded transmission. Short sayings and emblematic figures preserve much while revealing little to the unprepared.

The chapter thereby shows Pythagorean philosophy as symbolic education: concise forms carrying more than they first disclose.
