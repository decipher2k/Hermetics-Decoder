The chapter can now be read as an account of Pythagorean philosophy as attunement. Its doctrines, symbols, and disciplines all seek to tune the human being to hidden order.

Number and geometry train thought in relation. Music, silence, diet, friendship, and moral restraint train conduct in harmony. Aphorisms and symbols compress instruction so that it may be inwardly unfolded by the prepared disciple.

This education rests on correspondence. Man and cosmos share one image, so self-knowledge and world-knowledge deepen together. The Monad unifies this field while allowing graded orders of life and intelligence.

Transmigration, spiritual ascent, and philosophical astronomy all then become intelligible as aspects of one law: the soul moves according to what it resembles and understands.

The chapter's lasting claim is that wisdom is not merely taught. It is tuned into the whole human being by disciplined participation in number, symbol, and measured life.
