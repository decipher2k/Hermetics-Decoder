Extraction

Central statements
- The school at Crotona is presented as a graded initiatory community.
- Silence, secrecy, obedience, and degrees regulate access to deeper knowledge.
- Philosophy survives through disciplined communal transmission.

Operative qualities
Grading, secrecy, moral training, communal form, and initiatory transmission.

Reformulation

The chapter also makes clear that Pythagorean philosophy is institutional. Crotona is not simply a gathering of admirers but a structured order with degrees, tests, and divisions between outer and inner students. Access is earned through preparation.

Silence, secrecy, obedience, and progressive instruction are not arbitrary restrictions. They protect the integrity of the teaching and shape the aspirant into receptivity. Knowledge is given proportionately.

This aligns the chapter closely with the book's broader treatment of mystery schools. Pythagoras stands within the same lineage of guarded transmission, but he gives that structure a uniquely mathematical and ethical emphasis.

The school therefore becomes the social body of proportion, a community organized to embody what it teaches.
