This chapter presents Hermetic medicine as an integrated sacred science in which healing, philosophy, religion, cosmology, and natural observation form a single discipline. Hall argues that the art of healing originally belonged to temple wisdom because the human being was understood as more than a material body. Visible flesh is only the outer register of a layered organism informed by vital, astral, mental, moral, karmic, and celestial relations. Disease therefore cannot be fully understood at the level of tissue alone; it must be traced back to the disturbances in those subtler vehicles and patterns from which bodily disorder descends.

Paracelsus stands at the chapter's center because he recovers this wider medical horizon. Through the ideas of archæus, the universal life force, and mumia, the vehicle that carries and transmits that force, he explains how plants, talismans, foods, waters, sounds, prayers, and other therapeutic media can act upon the invisible channels through which life operates. The seven causes of disease and the seven corresponding methods of cure make clear that illness is multi-layered and that healing must be correspondingly plural. What matters is not a single favored remedy but the physician's capacity to identify where disorder has begun and what medium can reharmonize it.

The chapter's enduring teaching is that true medicine assists Nature by restoring order across levels of being. Symbolic efficacy and practical efficacy are not opposites here. Herbs heal through both material and subtle properties; sounds heal through both vibration and sacred pattern; prayer heals through relation to higher order; regimen heals by removing obstruction; and practical intervention has its place when wisely restrained. Healing is the re-establishment of right correspondence between the visible body and the invisible powers that sustain it.
