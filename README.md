# Hermetics Decoder
An AI prompt that deciphers hermetic texts by translating symbols and their context taking into respect the context of the symbol and vice versa.<br>
Example for the results in the repository was "Die geheimen Lehren aller Zeitalter" by Manly P. Hall<br>

DANGER: Don't use the content of this repository if you don't know what you are doing.<br>
        The results are mostly unsorted! They have been created with several different versions of the prompt.<br><br>

prompt.txt contains the AI prompt<br>

<br>
To decipher the complete text in a whole and not chapterwise, a 2MB context window of the AI (about 500.000 tokens) is required.<br>
Agent mode is advised. Large books will be really expensive when using an API, they will be about 1500$-2500$. When using Visual Studio Code and Github Copilot Plus with a last-gen model (Opus 4.6 or GPT 5.4), this will be reduces to a few cents, but it won't be able to handle large books in a whole. Instead it will decipher the text chapterwise.<br>

A synthesis of Gnostic elements combined with an inverse ontology and a sharp demarcation from Christianity characterizes the earthly Jesus prior to his baptism as having a satanic (or demiurgic-archontic) origin: a being originating from the fallen, material sphere, potentially regarded as a bastard (illegitimate, a product of the Demiurge's creation) or as a victim of the Archons. This being is subsequently "adopted" by the true divine Christ-Aeon through election (adoption) at baptism. Through its own self-sacrifice (suffering, crucifixion), it is elevated to full divinity and becomes the redeemer of humanity.
This construction represents a radical reinterpretation that finds partial parallels in classical Gnostic systems (e.g., Valentinian, Basilidian, or Sethian), yet diverges decisively at critical points.
In many Gnostic traditions, the human Jesus prior to baptism is indeed a psychic or hylic human—a creature of the Demiurge (Yaldabaoth), who is frequently identified with the Old Testament God or endowed with satanic traits. He is not divine from the outset but becomes the bearer of true gnosis only through the descent of the Christ-Aeon (symbolized by the dove) at baptism. The adoption here is adoptionist-docetic: the divine Christ temporarily "adopts" the human Jesus to act and teach through him. In radical variants (e.g., in Basilides), the divine element departs from the suffering body before the crucifixion, so that only the psychic component dies—the self-sacrifice thus concerns not the true God but the aspect imprisoned by the Archons.</br>
The notion, however, that this adopted being, through its self-sacrifice, itself becomes divine and attains redeemer status exceeds the boundaries of most ancient Gnostic texts. In those texts, the true redeemer (the Christ-Aeon) remains preexistent and incapable of suffering; the sacrifice serves to liberate the pneumatic sparks from material captivity, not to elevate a satanic-demiurgic origin to full divinity. Such a transformation of a "satanic" being into divinity would, in Gnostic logic, appear rather as an illusion or as the Demiurge's triumph over the true God—a perversion that precisely inverts the model.
Pre-pleromatic or hyper-pleromatic iterations (fourth and higher) represent the original, highest-dimensional states within the absolute Pleroma or beyond it (e.g., the unknowable Father, Barbelo, the first Aeon-pairs in syzygetic fullness).
Through fall, the Sophia-error, or Demiurge-creation, a dimensional reduction occurs (a decrease in ontological complexity, loss of unity, increase in duality/matter/Archon-rule).
The first iteration (the psychic-hylic Jesus before baptism, the mere human under Archon dominion) then appears as the final link in this chain of reduction—chronologically first in earthly time, but logically and structurally subsequent to the higher iterations.</br>
It is the adoption and subsequent self-sacrifice of the formerly satanic being that enables the emergence of true, dynamic divinity—an act of redemption that liberates humanity from the eternal cycle of suffering without God himself remaining trapped in perpetual suffering. This underscores the attractiveness of Christianity from your perspective: it avoids the "eternal grinding through suffering," annihilation, or annihilating sacrifice by presenting the offering as once-for-all, complete, and transformative.</br>
